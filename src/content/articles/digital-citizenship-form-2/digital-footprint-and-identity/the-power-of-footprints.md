# The Power of Footprints

*You already know what a footprint is — a mark you leave in the sand as you walk. But you are leaving footprints all day long without ever going to the beach. This article is about the footprints you leave online, and what happens to them. By the end you will be able to read your own trail like a map — and decide where you want it to lead.*

---

## 1. Every tap leaves a trace

When you walk across wet sand, your foot presses down and leaves a shape. When you use the internet — open an app, watch a video, send a message, tap a like — you leave a trace too. That trail of traces has a name: your **digital footprint**.

Your digital footprint is bigger than you might think. It includes:

- the sites you visit and the things you post;
- your comments, likes, and shares;
- the photos other people post *of you* or tag you in;
- data collected quietly in the background — where you are, what device you use, what you search for.

Some of that footprint is **active**: you deliberately put it there, like a post, a comment, or a form you filled in. Some of it is **passive**: it's collected automatically while you just use an app, without you posting anything at all. Either way, it's a trace.

---

## 2. Three words to remember: permanent, searchable, passable

Think of the difference between footprints in sand and footprints online. A footprint in sand is temporary — the next wave washes it away. Not your digital footprint. Digital footprints have three special powers, and they're worth memorising:

### Permanent — it doesn't wash away
Once something is online, it tends to stay. Even if you hit **delete**, other people may have already copied it, taken a screenshot, or shared it somewhere else — before you ever pressed the button. Old web pages can even be preserved in archives. Experts' rule of thumb: **assume anything you put online stays there forever.**

### Searchable — people can look you up
Anyone with an internet connection — a future teacher, a coach, a sports club, even an employer — can type your name into a search engine and find content you'd forgotten about, or thought was private. The first page of results under your name is a kind of **digital first impression** people form about you before you ever meet them.

### Passable — you don't control where it goes
Once you share something, you lose control of it. It can be copied, forwarded, and passed on to an **invisible audience** — people you never expected, and never meant, to see it. You might send a message to one friend; the whole year group might end up seeing it.

---

## 3. Worked example: the five-minute post

Let's build this out together, the way you'd solve a maths problem.

**The situation.** Last term, Ama posted an angry comment about a classmate during a bad day at school. Five minutes later she felt ashamed and deleted it.

**Step 1 — Ask: what was it made of?** Words, sent as a public comment.

**Step 2 — Ask: what happened before the delete button?** While the post was up, one person screenshotted it. In those five minutes, it spread to a group chat.

**Step 3 — Ask: is it really gone?** No. The screenshot survives. The comment can be re-shared tomorrow, next term, or next year.

**Step 4 — Ask: who could it reach?** Not just the classmate — their friends, Ama's friends, a future school or club that googles her name.

**The lesson.** Delete is not the same as *undo*. Ama's real mistake wasn't pressing delete too late — it was pressing *post* at all.

---

## 4. Footprints can be good or bad

Here's the part people forget: a digital footprint isn't *only* a danger. It can work for you.

A **positive footprint** might be your name on the football club results page, a video of you winning the school relay, a helpful comment you left on someone's project, or a post showing a hobby you love. These traces help people think well of you before you even speak.

A **negative footprint** might be a mean comment posted in anger, a risky photo, or something embarrassing that went viral. These traces can damage your **online reputation** — how people see you based on what appears about you online.

The same footprint can be both. The point isn't to have no footprints. It's to build footprints you'd be proud to have searched.

---

## 5. Who's looking — and what they see

Colleges, sports organisations, and employers do search people's names. Research is not fully settled on exactly *how many* recruiters check — older surveys gave numbers anywhere from about 43% to 93% depending on the study. But experts agree on the direction: many people will look, and a basic check of your name is common. So the safest and simplest rule for your age is: **assume someone will look — and keep your footprint something you'd be glad they found.**

One more reassuring note. If you're worried about an old mistake, you are not stuck with it. Recruiters say recent behaviour and proof that you've grown matter far more than old, one-off teenage mistakes. A footprint isn't a life sentence — but it does need looking after.

---

## 6. Not everything is in your hands

You might think, "I'm careful, so my footprint is fine." Not quite. Some of your footprint is made by other people:

- someone tags you in a photo you don't like;
- a friend posts about you without asking;
- a company collects your location data while you play a game;
- a stranger isn't who they claim to be.

Not everyone online who seems to be your age actually is. Apps may track where you are, and your data helps platforms build a profile of you to target adverts.

This doesn't mean you're powerless. It means part of digital citizenship is staying alert: check privacy settings with a trusted adult, think before you click, and remember that **you're not the only author of your footprint.**

---

## 7. The footprint audit

An **audit** means checking something carefully, the way you check your work for mistakes. A footprint audit is a systematic check of what's visible about you online. It's your own project — you build the picture of yourself, bit by bit.

### A worked example, step by step

1. **Search your own name** — in a private or incognito browser window, so your own search history doesn't skew the results. Write down the first page of what comes up.
2. **Note what you see.** Is it positive? Negative? Neutral? Outdated?
3. **Review old accounts.** That gaming account with the silly username you made when you were nine? Years later, that name can still surface in a search under your real name.
4. **Untag and ask.** Untag yourself from photos you don't like, and ask people who posted things about you to take them down.
5. **Tighten your settings.** Turn off location sharing for apps that don't need it, and review privacy settings with a trusted adult.

Do this with a parent, guardian, or teacher — they can help you spot things and action the parts you can't do alone.

---

## 8. One test before you post

Before you post anything, run the **cafeteria test**: if you wouldn't stand up and read it aloud in the middle of the school cafeteria at lunch, it probably shouldn't be posted. The people in that cafeteria are exactly the kind of invisible audience your post might reach — plus a few million you didn't count on.

## 9. Myth-busting corner

| "I heard..." | The truth |
|---|---|
| "If I delete it, it's gone forever." | Screenshots, copies, and archives survive deletion. |
| "My posts are private, so only friends see them." | Friends can copy and share, accounts can be hacked, and posts leak beyond their audience. |
| "I'm anonymous, so it can't be traced to me." | Platforms collect identifying data — IP address, device info, location — that can be linked to you. |
| "I'm too young for this to matter." | Clubs, schools, and employers search names, and old posts resurface years later. |
| "A bad post means I'll never get a job." | Growth and recent behaviour matter more than old mistakes — you can clean up and build positive content. |
| "Only what I post myself counts." | What others post about you, and data collected automatically, are part of your footprint too. |
| "The internet forgets." | Assume the opposite: anything you post online stays. |

---

## 10. Building your footprint, on purpose

Here's the constructionist secret — you are not just *finding out* about footprints. You are *building* one, every single day. Every post, comment, like, and upload is a brick in the wall of how the online world sees you.

The good news: you're the architect. Choose bricks you're proud of — your achievements, your hobbies, kindness to others — and the footprint you leave behind will be one you're happy to walk in.

---

## Key words

- **Digital footprint** — the record of everything about a person online, including what they post and what others post about them, created on purpose or by accident.
- **Permanent / persistent** — lasting a long time; something that doesn't go away.
- **Active footprint** — information you deliberately share, like a post, comment, or filled-in form.
- **Passive footprint** — information collected automatically as you use apps, sites, and devices.
- **Invisible audience** — people you didn't expect who might see your information, now or in the future.
- **Screen grab / screenshot** — capturing an image of the screen, so a copy survives even after content is deleted.
- **Online reputation** — how people view you based on what appears about you online.
- **Audit** — systematically checking what is visible about you online, like checking your work for mistakes.

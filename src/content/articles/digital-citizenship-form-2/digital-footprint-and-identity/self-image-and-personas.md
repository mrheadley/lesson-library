# Self-Image and Personas: Who Are You Online?

*Look in a mirror and you see yourself. But online, you might show a slightly different version of yourself — a version you choose. Maybe it looks a bit better, or funnier, or cooler than you feel in real life. This article is about those two versions of you: how they're built, what's fun about them, what can go wrong, and how to keep them honest. By the end, you'll be able to look at any profile — your own or someone else's — and see it for what it really is: a picture someone built.*

---

## 1. Two versions of you

Your **identity** is what makes you you: your background, your likes and dislikes, your hobbies, your values, your personality. It's the whole you.

Your **self-image** is how you feel about and see yourself — the story you tell yourself about who you are.

And then there's your **online persona**: the version of yourself you present on the internet. Your online identity is built from every post, like, comment, and photo you share. It's part of your overall identity — and it shapes how others see you both online *and* in person.

The key idea for this whole lesson: **your online self is a selection, not a photograph.** You choose which photos to show, what to post about, and which sides of yourself to reveal. Choosing carefully is called **curating** — the same word a museum uses for picking and arranging its best artworks.

---

## 2. The highlights reel

Imagine someone you know posts only perfect, filtered photos. In their feed, their life looks amazing — sunny holidays, great outfits, hilarious moments. Then the next day at school, they're just a normal kid, having a perfectly ordinary day.

What you saw was the **highlights reel**. People post their best moments, their best photos — often edited and filtered — not the full, ordinary picture of a life. This is normal, and it isn't a lie by itself. But it's a trap too: if you compare your everyday life to someone else's highlights, you're comparing your behind-the-scenes to their movie trailer. That comparison is unfair to you.

---

## 3. What a persona really is

A persona is the version of yourself you present online. It's not automatically a lie. Think of it as a *selected* version — like choosing your favourite photo of yourself to put in a frame, rather than every photo ever taken of you.

It only becomes a problem when it stops being a selection and becomes a **distortion**:

- when it's so far from the real you that other people feel misled;
- when it makes you feel you have to keep performing to keep it up;
- when it's used to deceive — like pretending to be someone else, or lying to strangers about who you are.

The line between a persona and a lie is honesty. A persona is fine. Impersonation — pretending to be another person to send mean messages, for example — is not playful role-play. That's harmful, and can be cyberbullying.

---

## 4. Why personas can be brilliant

It's tempting to think personas are all risk. They're not. Trying on different versions of yourself online is a completely normal part of growing up — many teenagers experiment with roles, avatars, usernames, and private "second" accounts. Here's what personas give you:

- **Control** — you get to choose how you present yourself.
- **Exploration** — a safe place to try out interests and express yourself.
- **Rehearsal space** — a practice ground for the person you want to become.
- **Creative freedom** — designing an avatar for a game, choosing a character, a skin, and a gamer tag. Usually, this is just harmless fun.

Designing an avatar that reflects your real interests — a hobby, your sense of humour — is a positive, healthy way to show your identity online. Personas are tools, and tools can be used well.

---

## 5. Where the risks live

For every benefit there's a flip side, and it helps to name it.

**Comparison.** Curated feeds show only highlights. When you scroll past a wall of "perfect" lives, it's easy to believe everyone else really is living that way. That's wrong — but believing it can hurt your self-esteem and how you feel about your own body.

**The filter trap.** Real research has recorded the words of a 10-year-old who said: *"I wish I was wearing a filter right now"* — unhappy with their real face compared to filtered versions of themselves. Filters are fun. They are also not how anyone actually looks. Comparing your real face to an edited image is comparing yourself to something that doesn't exist.

**The performance.** An idealised self that's far from the real you is linked to feeling less sure of who you are. Research suggests the more heavily curated and unauthentic the posting, the lower people's self-esteem — while posting positively but honestly tends to feel better.

**Being found out.** Online and offline are not two separate worlds. Friends at school see both. If your online persona clashes wildly with the real you, peers can discredit it — and the persona itself can be exposed.

Remember: the risk isn't having a persona. It's **deception, constant comparison, or performing only for approval.**

## 6. Worked example: the two accounts

Let's build this out together, the way you'd solve a problem.

**The situation.** Kwame has two accounts. On his main account, he posts to his school friends. On a private account — sometimes called a **finsta** — he posts more casually, only to a small trusted group.

**Step 1 — Ask: what is each account for?** Main account: public, broad, curated. Private account: small audience, more relaxed.

**Step 2 — Ask: is either one a lie?** Not necessarily. Both are *selected* versions of the real Kwame, shown to different audiences. That's a common, reasonable way to control who sees what.

**Step 3 — Ask: where could this go wrong?** If the public Kwame is so polished that friends at school don't recognise him. If he starts feeling he must keep performing to maintain the image. If either account becomes a place where he's dishonest about who he is.

**The lesson.** Having two versions is fine. The question is always: *is this version truthful, and am I comfortable with it?*

---

## 7. Reputation: the way people see you

Your online persona is one of the first things people use to form a judgment of you. That's your **online reputation** — how people view you based on what appears about you online.

Here's what the research says, and it's worth holding onto:

- A persona close to your true self tends to be **trusted** by friends.
- A persona that contradicts who you are can be **exposed** — and that exposure can damage trust.
- Being authentic online is linked to a **clearer sense of who you are**. Repeatedly presenting a made-up self is linked to feeling less sure of yourself.

It's not about being perfect online — nobody can be. It's about being **truthful and comfortable**. Quality beats polish: it's the quality of what you do online that matters most, not how much time you spend or how perfect it looks.

---

## 8. Two tests to use before you post

**The trusted-adult test.** Would your grandma, coach, or a trusted teacher be OK seeing this? If you'd be embarrassed if a trusted adult saw it, it's probably not the persona you want.

**The truthfulness test.** Is this version of me *me*? Or is it me pretending to be something I think will get more likes?

There's honest science here on the like-hunting question: some studies find peer approval matters a lot for teenagers, while others found most teens said follower counts didn't matter to them. The truth is somewhere in the middle — **some people are affected, and everyone responds differently.** That's why the useful question isn't "how do I get more likes?" It's "am I OK with the me I'm showing?"

---

## 9. Myth-busting corner

| "I heard..." | The truth |
|---|---|
| "A curated profile is the same as lying." | No — selecting what to share is normal. It becomes a problem only when it's dishonest or misleading. |
| "Everyone else really is living that perfect life." | They're posting edited highlights, not the full picture. |
| "My online persona is completely separate from my real life." | Online and offline identities are blended — school friends see both, and a persona that clashes with the real you can be discredited. |
| "The more perfect my profile, the better I'll feel." | Research suggests the opposite: heavy, unauthentic curation is linked to lower self-esteem. |
| "Filters and edits are just fun, so they can't hurt anyone." | Comparing your real face or body to edited images creates unfair, unachievable standards. |
| "Being different online is always dangerous." | Trying on different roles online is normal identity exploration — the concern is deception, comparison, or performance that hurts you or others. |
| "If I'm anonymous, I can say anything without consequences." | Even anonymous accounts can be traced, and hurtful behaviour damages your reputation and others' wellbeing. |

---

## 10. Building the you you're proud of

Here's the constructionist view: your online identity isn't something handed to you. It's something you **build**, day by day, choice by choice — the same way you'd build anything worth making.

You don't have to be perfect. You don't have to match anyone's highlights reel. You just have to build honestly — a version of you that's recognisable to the people who matter, and comfortable for you to wear.

And if something online ever makes you feel uncomfortable, sad, or unsure — about your identity or someone else's — that's a sign to pause and **talk to a trusted adult.** Asking for help isn't weakness. It's part of building well.

## Key words

- **Identity** — what makes you you: your background, likes, dislikes, hobbies, values, and personality.
- **Self-image** — the way you feel about and see yourself.
- **Persona / curated self** — the version of yourself you present online, made by selecting what to share and how to show it.
- **Curate** — to select, organise, and look after a collection (photos, posts, videos).
- **Online presence** — everything about you that people can see or find online.
- **Online reputation** — how people view you based on what appears about you online.
- **Avatar** — an online character or image that represents you.
- **Finsta** — a private or secondary account used to post to a smaller, trusted group, often more casually.
- **Authentic** — being true to who you really are, rather than only showing what others want to see.

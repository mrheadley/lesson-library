# Platform Workshop: Set Up a Blog

## Build It, Then Understand It

Reading about platforms teaches concepts. **Building on a platform teaches skills.** This workshop walks through the practical steps of setting up a blog — choosing a platform, configuring it, creating content, customising appearance, and testing across devices. The goal is not a perfect blog; it is a working blog and the confidence to develop it further.

## Choosing Your Platform

Your platform choice should match your goal:

- **Personal or hobby blog**: Blogger (free, zero setup, Google account required)
- **Professional portfolio**: WordPress.com or Squarespace (polished templates, easy customisation)
- **SEO-focused blog**: WordPress.org (maximum control, requires hosting)
- **Newsletter-driven blog**: Substack or Ghost (built-in subscription tools)

For this workshop, **WordPress.com** is the recommended starting point — it balances ease of use with enough power to grow.

## The Workshop Workflow

The repeatable workflow for any platform setup follows six stages:

**Choose → Configure → Create → Customise → Test → Share**

### Step 1: Create Your Account

For WordPress.com, sign up at wordpress.com. A free account gets you a `yourname.wordpress.com` subdomain. If you have a Google for Education account (common in Caribbean schools), you can use it for authentication, simplifying the login process.

For Blogger, sign in with your Google account, choose a template, and you are ready — Blogger is the fastest path from zero to published.

### Step 2: Configure Settings

Before creating content, set the foundational details:

- **Site Title**: Your blog's name. Keep it short and memorable.
- **Tagline**: A one-line description. "Photography, travel, and Caribbean stories" tells visitors what to expect.
- **Navigation**: Create pages for Home, About, and Blog. The Blog page will display your posts in reverse chronological order.
- **Permalink Structure**: Set this early. A descriptive structure like `/blog/post-name/` is better than `/blog/?p=123` for SEO and readability.

### Step 3: Select a Theme

Themes control visual appearance and layout. WordPress.com offers hundreds of free and premium themes. For a clean, professional start:

- Browse themes by category (personal, portfolio, business)
- Preview on desktop and mobile before selecting
- Avoid overly complex themes with features you will not use

You can always change your theme later without losing content.

### Step 4: Create Your First Post

The first post is a practice run — it teaches you the publishing workflow without the pressure of perfection.

**Structure for the first post:**

1. **Heading**: A clear, descriptive title. "My First Post: Why I Started This Blog" works better than "Hello World."
2. **Image**: Add at least one image. Compress it first using TinyPNG or Squoosh — large images cause slow load times. A 5 MB photo should become under 500 KB.
3. **Body Text**: Write 200–400 words about a topic you care about. A Caribbean recipe with step-by-step photos is engaging and practical.
4. **Call-to-Action**: End with a prompt — "What topic should I cover next? Leave a comment below."

### Step 5: Create an About Page

An About page is the most visited page on any blog. It should include:

- Your name
- What you do (e.g., "Form 6 Digital Media student at [school name]")
- What the blog covers
- How to contact you

This is essential for professional credibility. Visitors decide whether to stay based on whether they trust the person behind the content.

### Step 6: Customise Appearance

Adjust the visual presentation:

- Upload a profile image or logo
- Set accent colours that match your brand
- Configure the footer with copyright information
- Add social media links if applicable

Keep customisation minimal on the first pass. Function beats aesthetics on day one.

### Step 7: Test on Mobile

**Most readers will view your blog on their phones.** After setting up, open your blog on both a school computer and a personal smartphone. Check:

- Does the text read without zooming?
- Do images resize correctly?
- Does the navigation menu work on a small screen?
- Is the load time acceptable (under 3 seconds)?

Common mobile issues include images that overflow their containers, text that is too small, and menus that do not collapse into a hamburger icon.

### Step 8: Share the URL

Publishing finishes the setup, not the work. Share your blog URL with classmates and teachers. Feedback at this stage reveals problems you cannot see yourself — broken links, confusing navigation, or content that does not connect with your audience.

## Image Compression: A Critical Step

Caribbean internet connections often have bandwidth constraints. Large images are the most common cause of slow page loads. Before uploading any image:

1. Open TinyPNG (tinypng.com) or Squoosh (squoosh.app)
2. Upload your image
3. Download the compressed version
4. Upload the compressed file to your blog

A reduction from 5 MB to under 500 KB is typical with negligible visual quality loss. This single step can cut page load time from 8 seconds to under 3.

## SSL and Security

SSL certificates (HTTPS) encrypt data between the browser and server. You will see a padlock icon in the browser address bar when SSL is active. WordPress.com and Blogger include SSL free by default. If you use WordPress.org with custom hosting, ensure SSL is configured — browsers now display "Not Secure" warnings for HTTP sites, which destroys visitor trust.

## Common Misconceptions

### "Setting up a blog requires coding knowledge"
Modern platforms use drag-and-drop editors and one-click installers. Zero coding is needed to launch a functional, professional-looking blog.

### "The first post must be perfect"
The purpose of the first post is to learn the publishing workflow. You can always edit or replace it. Perfection is the enemy of progress — publish, then improve.

### "A free blog looks unprofessional"
With a good theme and clean content, a free WordPress.com or Blogger blog can look polished. Professionalism comes from content quality and presentation, not from the URL.

### "Publishing the post finishes the work"
Sharing the URL, checking analytics, responding to comments, and iterating on content are ongoing tasks. Publishing is the beginning of the content lifecycle.

### "Blogging is outdated because of social media"
A blog gives you **owned content** that social media algorithms cannot suppress or remove. Your social posts exist at the platform's pleasure; your blog exists on your terms.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| One-Click Installer | A hosting feature that automatically installs CMS software with a single button press |
| Static Front Page | A WordPress setting where a specific page appears as the homepage instead of the blog feed |
| Permalink | The permanent URL structure of a blog post, which should be descriptive and keyword-rich |
| SSL Certificate | A digital certificate encrypting browser-server data, indicated by the padlock icon and HTTPS |
| Theme | A pre-designed template controlling the visual appearance and layout of a website |
| Plugin | A software add-on extending CMS functionality (SEO, contact forms, analytics) |
| Call-to-Action | A prompt encouraging the reader to take a specific next step |

## Summary

Setting up a blog follows a repeatable workflow: choose a platform, configure settings, create content, customise appearance, test on mobile, and share the URL. WordPress.com offers the best balance of ease and power for most students. Compress images before uploading, create an About page for credibility, and test across devices. The first post is a learning exercise, not a masterpiece — publish, gather feedback, and iterate. A blog gives you owned content that no algorithm can take away.

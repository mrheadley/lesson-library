# Blog Deployment: WordPress, Blogger, Medium

## Three Platforms, Three Philosophies

Every blogging platform embodies a philosophy about what blogging should be. WordPress believes you should own everything. Blogger believes simplicity should be free. Medium believes distribution matters more than control.

Understanding these philosophies helps you choose the platform that matches your goals — not just your current needs, but where you're heading.

## WordPress: Ownership and Control

WordPress powers over 40% of all websites on the internet. It comes in two versions, and the distinction matters:

**WordPress.org** (self-hosted) — you download the software, install it on your own hosting, and control everything. Full access to 60,000+ plugins, 12,000+ free themes, and complete customisation. This is the platform for publishers who want full ownership.

**WordPress.com** (hosted) — a service run by Automattic that hosts WordPress sites on their infrastructure. The free tier is limited; paid plans ($4–45/month) unlock features progressively.

### WordPress.org Setup Path

1. **Buy hosting** — providers like SiteGround, Bluehost, or Cloudways ($5–30/month)
2. **Install WordPress** — most hosts offer one-click installation
3. **Choose a theme** — 12,000+ free themes in the repository, or purchase a premium theme
4. **Install plugins** — essential plugins include Yoast SEO (search optimisation), Wordfence (security), and WPForms (contact forms)
5. **Configure** — set permalinks, create pages, configure reading settings
6. **Publish** — create content using the Gutenberg block editor

### The Gutenberg Block Editor

WordPress 5.0 introduced Gutenberg, a block-based editor where each element — paragraph, image, quote, video — is a separate block. This is more intuitive than the old text editor and produces cleaner HTML output.

Think of it like building with LEGO: each block is a discrete element you position on the page. You can nest blocks, reorder them, and customise each one individually.

### SEO Control

WordPress gives you full SEO control through plugins like **Yoast SEO** or **Rank Math**. These tools help you:
- Set custom meta titles and descriptions for every page
- Generate XML sitemaps for search engine indexing
- Configure schema markup for rich results
- Analyse readability and keyword density
- Set canonical URLs to avoid duplicate content penalties

This level of SEO control is what separates WordPress from every other platform on this list. When someone Googles your name, your WordPress site can rank — because you control every signal search engines use.

### Monetisation

WordPress imposes no limits on monetisation. Advertising, affiliate links, e-commerce (WooCommerce), memberships, digital products, sponsored content — you choose the model, you keep the revenue, you control the terms.

### Analytics

WordPress uses Google Analytics via plugin, providing complete visitor data — traffic sources, geographic distribution, device types, behaviour flow, conversion tracking. You own this data; it lives on your server.

## Blogger: Simplicity by Google

Blogger is Google's free blogging platform. You sign in with a Google account, create a blog, pick a template, and start writing. The entire setup takes minutes.

### Setup

1. Go to blogger.com
2. Sign in with your Google account
3. Create a blog — choose a name and a URL (yourblog.blogspot.com)
4. Pick a template from the available options
5. Start writing and publishing

That's it. No hosting to configure. No plugins to install. No security patches to apply. Google handles everything.

### What You Get

- Completely free hosting with no ads on your content
- Google's infrastructure for speed and reliability
- Built-in Google Analytics integration
- Basic but functional editor
- Automatic mobile responsiveness

### What You Don't Get

- Custom design options are limited
- No plugin ecosystem
- Basic SEO capabilities (auto-generated sitemap, limited meta control)
- Google AdSense is your only monetisation option
- Limited to Google's template designs

### The Google Factor

Blogger is maintained by Google, which means it will likely exist for a long time — Google has no incentive to shut down a product that costs them little to maintain. But it also means you're entirely within Google's ecosystem. If Google changes policies, your blog is subject to those changes.

### Caribbean Context

Blogger is the ideal starting point for a Caribbean student with no budget and no technical experience. A blog about local culture, CSEC study tips, or a school project can be live within an hour. The zero-cost barrier makes it accessible regardless of financial circumstances.

## Medium: Distribution Without Control

Medium is a publishing platform with a built-in audience of millions of readers. You don't build an audience on Medium — you access one. This is its primary value proposition.

### Setup

1. Go to medium.com
2. Create an account (Google or Facebook sign-in)
3. Start writing in the editor
4. Publish to Medium's built-in network

The editor is clean, distraction-free, and designed for writing. No design decisions. No plugin choices. No configuration. Write and publish.

### The Medium Editor

Medium's editor prioritises writing quality. It handles formatting automatically — pull quotes, images, embeds, and responsive layouts are built in. You focus on content; Medium handles presentation.

This constraint is both a strength and a limitation. You cannot customise the design. You cannot add custom functionality. You write within Medium's framework, and Medium displays it as it sees fit.

### SEO and Discovery

Medium's domain authority means articles can rank well on Google — but the SEO benefit goes to medium.com, not to your personal brand. When someone searches for your name and finds your Medium article, the URL is medium.com/@yourname, not yoursite.com.

Medium's internal algorithm determines which articles get promoted in feeds and recommendations. You don't control this algorithm. A change in it can dramatically affect your visibility.

### The Partner Program

Medium's Partner Program pays writers based on how much time paying members spend reading their articles. Earnings vary widely — most Medium writers earn very little. It supplements income rather than replacing it. Think of it as a bonus, not a business model.

### What Medium Doesn't Offer

- No email list building — you cannot collect subscriber emails
- No custom domain on the free tier (paid: $5/month for custom domain)
- No analytics beyond basic internal stats
- No monetisation beyond the Partner Program
- No control over design or layout

### The Canonical Tag

If you publish on Medium and also maintain your own website, use a **canonical tag** to tell search engines which version is the original. This prevents Medium's higher domain authority from outranking your own site for your own content. WordPress plugins like Yoast handle this automatically.

## Platform Comparison

| Feature | WordPress.org | Blogger | Medium |
|---------|--------------|---------|--------|
| Cost | $5–30/mo hosting | Free | Free (Partner Program) |
| Setup time | Hours to days | Minutes | Minutes |
| Design control | Unlimited | Limited | None |
| SEO control | Full | Basic | Internal algorithm only |
| Monetisation | Unlimited | AdSense only | Partner Program |
| Analytics | Google Analytics | Google Analytics | Basic internal |
| Content ownership | Full | Google-controlled | Medium-controlled |

## Choosing Based on Primary Goal

- **Own your audience and brand** — WordPress.org. Full control, full ownership, full SEO benefit.
- **Write with zero friction** — Medium. The audience exists. The editor is clean. Accept that you don't control the platform.
- **Start a blog today for free** — Blogger. Zero cost, live in minutes, Google-backed reliability.
- **School project with professional appearance** — WordPress.com paid tier ($4–8/month) or Squarespace.

## Common Misconceptions

### "Medium articles rank well on Google for your name"
Medium's domain authority means the article may rank, but the SEO value goes to Medium, not your personal brand. The URL is medium.com/@yourname, not yoursite.com. For personal branding, this matters.

### "WordPress.com and WordPress.org are the same thing"
WordPress.com is hosted and limited; WordPress.org is self-hosted with full control. They share the same underlying software but offer fundamentally different experiences and capabilities.

### "Blogger is outdated and not worth using"
It remains the best zero-cost option for students who want a personal blog with no financial commitment. Google maintains it, it works reliably, and it requires no technical knowledge.

### "You should only pick one platform"
A multi-platform strategy — own site plus Medium plus social media — maximises reach while building equity. Publish on your own site first, then syndicate to other platforms with canonical tags.

### "Medium pays well for writers"
Most Medium writers earn very little; it supplements rather than replaces income. Treat it as distribution, not as a revenue source.

### "WordPress is too complex for a simple blog"
WordPress can be as simple or as complex as you need. A basic blog with a default theme requires minimal configuration. The complexity scales with your ambitions, not with the platform's requirements.

## Summary

WordPress, Blogger, and Medium represent three distinct philosophies: ownership, simplicity, and distribution. WordPress offers full control and SEO capability but requires setup time and hosting investment. Blogger is free and immediate but limited in design and customisation. Medium provides built-in audience access but sacrifices control over design, monetisation, and audience data. The right choice depends on your primary goal: building a brand (WordPress), writing with zero friction (Medium), or starting today for free (Blogger). For long-term publishing, own your domain regardless of which platform you choose — it's the portable foundation that travels with you.

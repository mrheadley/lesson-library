# App Marketing Strategy Development: One Plan Across the Whole Journey

## Strategy vs Campaign

Before anything else, separate two things:

- **A campaign** (L151/152) is one *coordinated, time-boxed action* within the wider plan.
- **A strategy** (this lesson) is the *long-term, phased, whole-product* plan that organises all the campaigns and channels.

The strategy is the umbrella. Guerrilla (L148), owned email (L149), influencer/paid (L150), ASO (L155), social (L156), and content (L157) all slot into it. This lesson is where you assemble them into one coherent, phased, measurable plan.

## The Phased Strategy: Pre-Launch, Launch, Post-Launch

The organising frame is **three phases**, each with different goals and levers:

### Pre-launch (build momentum)
- Build a **waiting list / community** and capture email (L149).
- Run **teasers** and prepare a strong store listing/ASO (L155).
- Line up **influencers/creators** (L150).
- Design **share/viral mechanics** (L148).

The goal: launch-day has momentum and a place to send eager users.

### Launch (the burst)
A concentrated, coordinated burst across paid/influencer (L150), social (L156), content (L157), and the store submission (L134/154) — designed to spike **install velocity**, which in turn lifts ranking and visibility (L155).

### Post-launch (retain and scale)
- **Retention and re-engagement** (email/win-back — L149).
- A steady **content cadence** (L157).
- Respond to **reviews/ratings** (L155).
- **Scale the channels that measured best** (L151/158).

## The User-Acquisition Funnel: The Strategy's Backbone

Underneath the phases is a **user-acquisition funnel** — the stage-by-stage path every user travels, and at which you optimise a specific lever:

```
Awareness → View (store page) → Install → Activation (first value)
         → Retention (repeat use) → Revenue / Advocacy
```

The crucial discipline: **assign a tool to each stage**, and do not confuse stages. Cheap awareness ("get views") is not success; the revenue stage is.

## The Trap the Funnel Exposes: Acquisition ≠ Success

The funnel makes the biggest strategy mistake visible. A strategy optimised **only for installs** ("get downloads") but weak at **activation or retention** produces high installs and heavy churn. **Acquisition is not the same as success.** Retention is part of the strategy, not an afterthought. A launch that fills your device with users who never return is a failed strategy, even if installs look impressive.

## CAC vs LTV: Is the Strategy Sustainable?

User acquisition has a **cost**, and value must exceed it:

- **CAC** — cost per acquisition: average marketing spend to acquire one user.
- **LTV** — lifetime value: the value a user generates over their lifetime (retention × monetisation).

The decision rule: **a model is sustainable only when LTV > CAC.** This is also the bridge into the revenue-models thread coming next — marketing and revenue are two halves of the same business decision.

## A Living Document, Not a Script

The strategy is **adaptive, not a rigid checklist**. It includes **review points** at set dates:

- Check the funnel metrics.
- **Cut** underperforming channels.
- **Double down** on winners.

This is the course's "measure → adjust → remeasure" and "necessary friction" in action — you plan, execute, read the data, and adapt. A strategy you never adjust is a guess you've committed to.

## Regional Realism

The three-phase structure is universal, but the content of each phase is region-weighted:

- **Pre-launch:** WhatsApp/community-led capture, not just email.
- **Launch:** data-light mechanics, timed to a local event (carnival, school term).
- **Post-launch:** retention via the platforms local users actually use.

A regional brief (like the workshop's) forces this; a global template that assumes unlimited data and a different platform mix fails the regional test.

## Worked Example: Assigning the Funnel

For a school study app, assign a tool to each funnel stage:

- **Awareness** → short-form video + a campus guerrilla stunt (L156/148)
- **Store page view** → ASO keywords and captions (L155)
- **Install** → a launch burst = paid/influencer push (L150/158)
- **Activation** → in-app onboarding (first "aha" fast)
- **Retention** → email/win-back + a weekly content tip (L149/157)
- **Revenue/advocacy** → "invite a friend" + (next lessons) a monetisation choice

Each stage has a named tool and a KPI. That is the strategy as a working map, not a slogan.

## Common Misconceptions

### "Marketing strategy starts at launch."
Pre-launch (buzz, community, waitlist, ASO, influencers) largely determines launch-day momentum. A solid strategy begins well before the listing goes live.

### "Getting installs is the whole job."
The funnel shows install is the middle, not the end. An install-heavy/retention-weak strategy churns users and fails — activation and retention are part of the strategy.

### "A strategy is a fixed checklist to follow."
It's a live, data-driven plan with review points. Iterate — cut what fails, double down on what works — rather than rigidly executing.

### "The strategy and the campaign are the same thing."
A campaign (L151/152) is one time-boxed action within the longer-term phased strategy (L158). Confusing the two misplans effort.

### "Every channel belongs in every phase."
Tools are phase/position-specific — pre-launch buzz differs from launch-burst paid differs from post-launch email retention. Placing them wrong wastes the strategy.

### "A global strategy template works for every app."
The phases are universal, but the channel mix within each is region-weighted (Caribbean WhatsApp/data reality). Localise the template.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Marketing strategy | The long-term, phased, whole-product plan that organises all campaigns and channels |
| Phases (pre/launch/post) | The three strategic periods, each with distinct goals and levers |
| User-acquisition funnel | The stage path (awareness → revenue) measured separately at each stage |
| Install velocity | The download rate — a ranking signal and reason for the launch burst |
| CAC / LTV | Cost per acquisition vs lifetime value; sustainable when LTV > CAC |
| Iteration loop | The measure → adjust → remeasure cycle that makes the strategy a living document |

## Summary

A marketing strategy is the phased, whole-product umbrella over every campaign and channel. Organise it into pre-launch (momentum), launch (a velocity burst), and post-launch (retention + scale), and drive it with a user-acquisition funnel — assigning a tool and a KPI to each stage. Guard against the biggest trap (acquisition ≠ success): retention is part of the strategy. Keep it sustainable by keeping LTV > CAC, keep it adaptive with review points, and localise the channel mix for the region. One plan, one journey, measured and iterated.

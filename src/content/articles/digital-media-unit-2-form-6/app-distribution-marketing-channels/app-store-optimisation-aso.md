# App Store Optimisation (ASO): Being Found, Being Installed

## Two Jobs, One Discipline

**ASO** — **App Store Optimisation** — is the practice of optimising an app's store listing so it ranks higher in search and converts more browsers into installs. It is "the SEO of the app store." It has two distinct jobs, and you must keep them separate:

1. **Visibility** — being *found* when people search (ranking higher → more impressions).
2. **Conversion** — turning someone who *finds* the listing into an *install*.

A listing can rank high but convert badly (wrong message), or convert well but never be found (no keywords). ASO works on both.

## Why ASO Is Worth Doing

The app store is a **primary discovery channel** — and the traffic it generates is **organic** (free). A well-optimised listing generates free installs that *compound* with the paid and influencer channels you arrange around it. A strong effect used in teaching: effective ASO is associated with a roughly **4–5× boost in organic downloads** versus an unoptimised listing. That is a motivating, concrete number — and it is why ASO belongs in the launch plan, not as an afterthought.

## Visibility Factors (Getting Found)

The store's ranking algorithm is not fully public, but its inputs are observable and improvable:

- **Title / name** with a target keyword
- **Subtitle / keyword field** (especially Google Play's dedicated keyword field)
- **Relevance** of your metadata to the search query
- **Install velocity** — the download rate over time (why launch momentum matters)
- **Engagement** — users who install and stay signal quality

These are the levers that raise your *position* in search results.

## Conversion Factors (Getting Installed)

Once someone lands on your listing, these factors decide whether they install:

- **Icon** — your first impression
- **Screenshots** — often the **strongest conversion driver**
- **Preview / video** — showing real use
- **Ratings & reviews** — higher rating → higher trust and conversion
- **Description** — clearly showing the value

## The Sneaky One: Screenshots Affect Both Jobs

Modern store search indexes the **text inside screenshots** (Apple has done this via OCR-ised caption indexing since 2017). That means the *captions you write on your screenshots* are both a **conversion** factor and a **visibility/ranking** factor at once. This is the design-trend insight from the trends lessons, now applied: writing clear, searchable captions on screenshots improves both how high you rank and how likely someone is to install. It is a two-for-one you can act on immediately.

## Experimentation: A/B Testing Your Listing

ASO is **measured experimentation**, not a one-time setup. The discipline is:

1. Test one element at a time — an icon variant, a different screenshot order, a subtitle change.
2. Read the data — which variant converted better?
3. Keep the winner, and test the next element.

This is the same **measure → adjust → remeasure** loop as the strategy lessons, applied to the listing itself. That is why ASO is continuous, not a publish-and-forget task.

## Ratings: When You Ask Matters

Getting a higher rating is not automatic — *when and how you ask* matters. Smart apps:

- **Prompt for a rating after a positive moment** (a success, a completed task), not at first open.
- **Respond to reviews** — this improves brand image and perceived responsiveness.
- **Build early momentum** — early positive ratings boost credibility and conversion.

A great app with a bad rating-prompt timing converts poorly; a decent app asked at the right moment can do better. The rating is partly a product of the praming, not just the quality.

## The Organic-and-Paid Connection

ASO's organic traffic does not exist in a vacuum. The paid and influencer campaigns you run (a targeted influencer post, a launch burst) drive **install velocity**, which lifts your ranking, which generates more organic traffic. ASO and paid marketing feed each other: **paid drives velocity → velocity lifts rank → rank drives organic.**
## Worked Example: Optimising One Screenshot

For a school study app:

- The screenshot shows the app's plan view.
- **Unoptimised caption:** "Our app." (useless for search and for persuasion)
- **Optimised caption:** "Plan any term in minutes — works offline." (a searchable keyword phrase + a value claim)

That one caption now (a) matches the likely search "plan app" and (b) tells the browser the value. Same screenshot, two jobs done. Then A/B it against another caption and keep whichever converts better.

## Common Misconceptions

### "ASO is a one-time set-up when you publish."
No — ASO is continuous (monitor → A/B test → adjust). Rankings and conversion drift as search behaviour and competition change.

### "A catchy name is all it takes to rank."
Ranking depends on relevance to the query, keyword placement, install velocity, and engagement. A name alone rarely ranks.

### "Pretty screenshots are just decoration."
Screenshots are a primary conversion driver *and* their captions are indexed in search — both a conversion and a visibility factor.

### "A higher rating is guaranteed to come with a great app."
Ratings depend on when/how you ask, review responses, and early momentum. A great app with a bad rating-prompt timing converts poorly.

### "ASO replaces marketing/advertising."
ASO generates valuable organic traffic but is one channel. The strong outcome is ASO *compounding* with the paid/influencer campaigns that drive install velocity.

### "The ranking algorithm is a mystery I can't affect."
Its inputs (keywords, velocity, ratings, retention) are observable and improvable via measured A/B testing. You steer it by experiment, not by guessing.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| ASO | Continuously optimising a listing's visibility (rank) and conversion (installs) |
| Organic traffic/installs | Installs from store search/browse, not paid — ASO's main lever |
| Metadata | Title, subtitle, keyword field, description that tell the store (and browser) what the app is |
| Install velocity | The download rate over time — a ranking signal |
| Conversion rate (listing) | % of page-viewers who install — what ASO conversion work optimises |
| A/B test (ASO) | Running two versions of an element and keeping the better-converting one |
| Visual/search indexing | Store search reading text inside screenshots/captions — visuals affect both rank and conversion |

## Summary

ASO has two jobs — visibility (being found) and conversion (being installed) — and works on both through metadata, visuals, ratings, and experimentation. Titles/keywords and install velocity raise your rank; icon, screenshots, video, and ratings convert viewers. Screenshot captions affect *both* jobs at once because store search reads them, and A/B testing lets you continuously improve. Drive install velocity with paid/influencer campaigns and ASO compounds into organic traffic. It is a continuous, measured discipline — the SEO of the app store.

# App Marketing Workshop: Plan a Campaign

## The Point of the Workshop

Everything in Section 7's distribution and marketing lessons (L154–158) now comes together into **one deliverable**: a complete marketing plan for a mobile app. This is the *doing* of the strategy thread — the workshop where you prove you can *produce* an integrated plan, not just recall the concepts.

The workshop uses a **brief**: a product scenario plus explicit constraints (budget, audience, region). Your job is to build a plan that integrates all five pieces:

1. **Distribution model** (L154) — store, direct, enterprise, sideload + why
2. **ASO / listing** (L155) — keywords, screenshots/captions, ratings
3. **Channel plan** (L156–157) — social (video-forward) + content (calendar)
4. **Phased strategy** (L158) — pre/launch/post + the funnel
5. **Funnel KPIs & review** (L158) — the numbers you'll check and when

This is not a single-channel sketch — it is the full integrated plan.

## The One-Page Plan Discipline

The workshop is time-boxed (roughly 25 minutes of build time in a 35-minute period), so the plan must be **buildable in one period** — the discipline of a **one-page plan**, not an essay. Work to a checklist so completeness beats polish:

> **Minimum viable plan:** goal → persona → message → channels → timing → budget → KPIs → review point.

Tick each box as you fill it. A tight one-pager that covers all eight beats a sprawling, unintegrated document — and the checklist doubles as the grading rubric.

## Small Budget Is the Point

Deliberately, the **budget is small**. This forces the real trade-offs rather than letting you "buy" success:

- Lead with **owned** (email/content) — near-zero cost (L149)
- Use a **guerrilla** stunt or two — low-cost (L148)
- Spend a little on one or two **targeted paid/influencer** plays where relevance is high (L150)

If you could spend unlimited money, the wise choices would be hidden. The small budget makes you think about audience and priorities — and it mirrors reality for a school or local app.

## Make It Regional

The best briefs are **local** — a school, community, or news app for one island. A regional brief forces decisions that fit the Caribbean's actual data/platform reality:

- Weight **WhatsApp/community** and **data-light content**.
- Schedule the launch burst around a **local event** (carnival, school term).
- Choose channels by where local users actually are.

A copy-pasted global template visibly fails a regional brief — which is itself the lesson. If your plan would be identical for a market on another continent, it is probably not a real plan.

## The Funnel Is Your Guard

Use the funnel from the strategy lesson as a check. For each **stage** (awareness → store view → install → activation → retention), name a **tool and a KPI**:

| Stage | Tool | KPI |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Short-form video + guerrilla stunt | Reach |
| Store view | ASO keywords/captions | Page views |
| Install | Launch burst (paid/influencer) | Installs |
| Activation | In-app onboarding | % activated |
| Retention | Email/win-back + content cadence | Retention rate |

This guards against the classic trap: a plan obsessed with installs and silent about activation/retention is a plan that will churn users. If your plan cannot name a retention tool, it is incomplete.

## Worked Example: The Checklist in Action

A group gets the brief "school study app, £40, one island." They produce:

- **Goal (SMART):** 500 installs, ≥35% activation, within 4 weeks, £40.
- **Persona:** local students 16–18 on campus.
- **Message:** "Study faster, offline."
- **Distribution:** public store (reach + trust for a public app).
- **ASO:** "plan" + "offline" in title/caption; A/B an icon.
- **Channels:** owned email (existing), a campus guerrilla display, one relevant local micro-influencer.
- **Timing:** pre-launch 1 week → launch 3-day burst → post weekly content.
- **Budget:** £5 guerrilla + £30 influencer + £5 misc.
- **KPIs:** 500 installs, 35% activation, CTR per channel.
- **Review:** check funnel metrics at week 2; cut what fails, double down on what works.

That plan is complete against the checklist, integrated, regional, and measurable — the whole workshop's goal.

## The Share-Out

A short share-out closes the workshop: each group presents its plan in under a minute — the one-persona goal, the phase plan, and one KPI. This practises the concise, evidence-based communication from the trends workshop, and it lets the class learn from each other's plans.

## Common Misconceptions

### "A marketing plan is just the promotion part."
An integrated Section 7 plan also includes the distribution model (L154) and the store listing/ASO (L155). Promotion is one layer, not the whole plan.

### "Longer plans are better plans."
The workshop rewards completeness against the checklist and integration, not length. A tight one-pager covering goal/channels/timing/budget/KPIs/review beats a sprawling, unintegrated one.

### "Budget is unlimited in a plan."
The small, fixed budget forces prioritisation and regional realism. Spending your way out of a weak decision is the easy (and wrong) answer.

### "The plan just needs a good slogan."
The plan needs measurable objectives and a funnel view. A catchy message without KPIs or a funnel assignment isn't a plan.

### "A global template is fine for a local app."
The regional brief forces WhatsApp/data-light/local-event decisions. A copy-paste global plan fails the brief's realism test.

### "A finished plan is a finished strategy."
The plan includes a review point and iteration loop (L158). It's a living document, not a one-time artefact to file away.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Marketing plan (workshop) | One integrated one-page document covering distribution, ASO, channels, phases, budget, KPIs, review |
| Minimum viable plan | The essential checklist (goal → persona → message → channels → timing → budget → KPIs → review) |
| Integrated plan | A plan combining distribution (L154), ASO (L155), and the channel/strategy tools (L156–158) |
| Brief | The product scenario + explicit constraints that bound the planning task |
| Funnel KPIs | Per-stage metrics (awareness → retention) the plan promises to measure |

## Summary

This workshop consolidates Section 7 into a single deliverable: a one-page integrated marketing plan. Build it against the checklist (goal → persona → message → channels → timing → budget → KPIs → review), integrating distribution, ASO, channels, and the phased strategy. Keep the budget deliberately small to force the real trade-offs, make the brief regional so global templates fail, and use the funnel to guard against an install-obsessed, retention-blind plan. Finish with a concise share-out. Completeness, integration, and regional realism beat length — that is the workshop's standard.

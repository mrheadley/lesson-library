# Hosted and Online Publishing Platforms

You have probably read a blog, watched a YouTube video, or opened an e-book on your phone this week. What you may not have thought about is the platform sitting behind that content — the engine that lets a creator publish their work and lets you find it. Every time you consume digital content, you are interacting with a publishing platform, whether it is WordPress hosting a local business website, Medium carrying an essay from a stranger, or TikTok surfacing a cooking video from Trinidad. Understanding how these platforms work is the first step toward becoming a creator yourself rather than only a consumer.

This article walks through the major categories of hosted and online publishing platforms: blogging and writing platforms, video platforms, e-book publishing, and streaming. It explains how each one makes money, what it offers creators, and where the trade-offs lie. Throughout, we use Caribbean examples and context so that the content feels relevant to the world you actually live in — not just Silicon Valley.

---

## What Is a Hosted Publishing Platform?

Think of a hosted publishing platform like a stall in a market. The market owner (the platform) provides the space, the foot traffic, the security, and the payment systems. You, the creator, bring your goods (your content) and set up your stall. You do not have to build the market from scratch, but you also do not own the building, and the market owner can change the rules about where your stall sits or how much rent to charge.

A **hosted** platform means someone else manages the servers, the software updates, the security patches, and the infrastructure. You log in, create content, and hit publish. The alternative — a self-hosted solution — means you rent or buy your own server, install the software yourself, and maintain everything. Self-hosting gives you total control but also total responsibility.

For most beginners and for many professionals, hosted platforms are the right starting point. The question is which one, and that depends on what you want to achieve.

## Blogging and Writing Platforms

### WordPress.com

WordPress.com is the hosted version of WordPress. Do not confuse it with WordPress.org, which is the self-hosted version. The difference matters:

| Feature | WordPress.com | WordPress.org |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting | Managed by WordPress | You arrange your own |
| Cost | Free tier; paid plans from ~$4/month | Software free; hosting $3–$30/month |
| Customisation | Limited on free plan | Unlimited |
| Monetisation | Restricted on free plan | No restrictions |
| Technical skill needed | Minimal | Moderate to high |
| Domain name | yoursite.wordpress.com (free) | yoursite.com (you choose) |

WordPress.com offers built-in newsletter publishing, SEO tools, and a library of themes. It is the most practical choice if you want to start a blog or portfolio without touching a single line of code. The trade-off is that the free plan displays WordPress branding and limits what you can do with your site's design.

**Worked example:** Suppose you are a Form 6 student building an online portfolio of your photography. WordPress.com's free plan gives you a clean gallery layout, a customisable URL, and the ability to add pages for your work, your bio, and a contact form. If later you want your own domain name (say, `jamiephotos.com`), you upgrade to a paid plan. You never have to worry about servers going down or software updates — WordPress handles all of that.

### Blogger

Blogger is Google's free blogging platform. It ties directly to your Google account, so if you have Gmail, you are already set up. Blogger integrates with Google AdSense, meaning you can earn money from ads displayed on your blog.

The downsides are real, though. Blogger has a small selection of design templates, limited built-in discovery (nobody stumbling onto your blog through Blogger's search), and a general reputation as a platform that has not kept pace with newer alternatives. It is best for beginners who want to experiment at zero cost, or for personal projects where professional appearance is not the priority.

### Medium

Medium is less a platform you own and more a community you join. When you publish on Medium, your writing appears alongside thousands of other articles in a shared ecosystem. Medium's built-in readership means your work can be discovered by people who are already browsing the platform — something Blogger and WordPress.com do not offer.

The catch is significant: you do not own the audience. If you build 5,000 followers on Medium and then decide to leave, those followers stay with Medium. You cannot export your email list. You are essentially renting your audience rather than owning it.

Medium does pay writers through its Partner Program, where earnings are based on how much time paying members spend reading your work. This can provide modest income for popular writers, but it is not a reliable salary.

### Substack

Substack sits between a blog and an email newsletter. Creators publish content that lands directly in subscribers' inboxes — a powerful distribution method because email has no algorithm deciding who sees what. Substack supports free and paid subscriptions, with Substack taking a 10% cut of paid subscription revenue plus payment processing fees.

A critical advantage of Substack is that you own your email list. If you leave the platform, you take your subscribers with you. This is the opposite of Medium and is one reason serious newsletter creators often prefer Substack.

However, Substack has responsibilities that come with subscriber data. In February 2026, Substack confirmed a data breach affecting nearly 700,000 user records. This event illustrates an important lesson: even when you own your list, the platform's security practices still affect your audience.

Substack also requires users to be 18 or older for paid features, which is relevant for Form 6 students considering it as a publishing option.

### Choosing a Writing Platform

The right choice depends on your goal:

- **Want visibility and discovery?** Medium gives you access to an existing audience.
- **Want to build a paying community?** Substack lets you own the relationship and earn directly.
- **Want full ownership and long-term brand building?** WordPress.com (or eventually WordPress.org) gives you the most control.
- **Want to experiment for free with no commitment?** Blogger costs nothing and requires no technical knowledge.

## Video Platforms

### YouTube

YouTube is the dominant video platform globally and the most versatile. It supports long-form video, short-form content (Shorts), live streaming, and multiple monetisation layers:

- **Ad revenue:** You earn a share of advertising shown before or during your videos.
- **Channel memberships:** Subscribers pay a monthly fee for exclusive perks.
- **Super Chats:** Viewers pay to highlight messages during live streams.
- **YouTube Premium revenue:** A share of subscription fees from Premium users who watch your content.
- **Content ID:** Automated system that detects when others use your music or footage and allocates revenue accordingly.

To access YouTube's monetisation (the YouTube Partner Programme), you need at least 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours in the past 12 months. This threshold means most student creators will not earn revenue from classroom projects — but building an audience now pays off later.

The average revenue per 1,000 views (RPM) ranges from $1 to $5, with specialised niches earning $10 or more. For context, a Caribbean cooking channel with consistent uploads and a loyal audience can build toward meaningful income over time.

### TikTok

TikTok leads short-form video and is built around an algorithm that rewards trend participation and virality. The Creator Rewards Programme pays $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 views for videos longer than one minute — significantly less than YouTube's rates. Most TikTok creators rely on brand partnerships and sponsored content rather than platform payments alone.

The trade-off between YouTube and TikTok is straightforward: YouTube rewards watch time and subscriber loyalty, while TikTok rewards trend participation and virality. A YouTube channel is a long-term asset that compounds over years. A TikTok presence can explode overnight but is harder to sustain and monetise consistently.

### Vimeo

Vimeo positions itself as a professional alternative to YouTube. It emphasises creative quality and offers premium hosting, streaming, and portfolio features. Vimeo does not have YouTube's advertising model, so creators typically use it to host client work, sell video courses, or build subscription-based video services through Vimeo OTT (now Vimeo Streaming).

For Caribbean creators, Vimeo is less relevant for audience building but useful for professional portfolios and client deliveries.

## E-Book Publishing

If you have ever wanted to write a book and put it in front of readers worldwide, self-publishing platforms have made that possible without a traditional publishing deal.

### Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP)

KDP is the largest e-book publishing platform. You upload your manuscript, set your price, and Amazon distributes it to Kindle readers globally. KDP offers up to 70% royalty on sales, and your book appears in the world's biggest online bookstore.

KDP Select is a programme that requires 90-day exclusivity — your e-book cannot be sold on other platforms during that period. In exchange, you gain access to Kindle Unlimited (a subscription reading service) and promotional tools. After each 90-day period, you can choose to renew or withdraw and publish widely.

### Smashwords / Draft2Digital

Smashwords merged with Draft2Digital in 2022 but continues operating as a distinct brand. It distributes e-books to over 400 retailers and library systems, including Apple Books, Barnes & Noble, Kobo, and public libraries. Authors keep roughly 90% of revenue (Smashwords/D2D takes about 10% commission).

The key difference from KDP is distribution reach. KDP dominates Amazon but cannot reach Apple Books, Kobo, or libraries. Smashwords/D2D covers the non-Amazon ecosystem. Many authors use both: KDP for Amazon sales and Smashwords/D2D for everywhere else. You cannot combine KDP Select exclusivity with wide distribution.

**Worked example:** Imagine you write a short story collection rooted in Caribbean folklore. You publish the e-book through KDP Select for 90 days to test Amazon's market and use Kindle Unlimited promotion. After the exclusivity period ends, you remove it from KDP Select and publish through Smashwords/D2D to reach Apple Books, Kobo, and library systems. Your book is now available on every major platform.

## Streaming Platforms

Streaming has expanded beyond music into video, live broadcasting, and subscription content.

### YouTube Live

YouTube's live streaming capabilities allow creators to broadcast in real time, accept Super Chat donations, and offer channel memberships during live events. This is the most accessible live streaming option for Caribbean creators because it integrates with an existing YouTube channel and audience.

### TikTok Live

TikTok Live allows creators with 1,000 or more followers to broadcast live and receive virtual gifts that convert to currency. TikTok's algorithm often boosts live content during broadcasts, giving creators temporary visibility. However, the audience is ephemeral — once the live ends, discovery returns to the algorithm's standard behaviour.

### Vimeo Streaming (OTT)

Vimeo OTT enables creators to build subscription-based video-on-demand services. Instead of relying on ad revenue, you charge viewers directly for access to your content. This model suits organisations or creators with existing audiences willing to pay for exclusive material — for example, a Caribbean cooking school offering online classes.

## Caribbean Content Creators: Case Studies

The Caribbean has produced creators who demonstrate how cultural identity becomes a content strategy.

**Caribbean Pot (Chris De La Rosa, Trinidad and Tobago)** has spent over a decade building a YouTube following through Caribbean cooking content. His channel shows how niche cultural content — recipes rooted in Trinidadian and wider Caribbean cuisine — can reach global audiences hungry for authentic representation.

**Chef Ricardo (Jamaica)** helped pioneer Caribbean cooking on YouTube, using approachable recipes to attract home cooks worldwide. His success demonstrated that Caribbean food culture has international appeal far beyond the region.

**Dale Elliott Jr. (Jamaica)** built a comedy platform rooted in Jamaican life, crossing into mainstream entertainment while maintaining Caribbean identity. His content proves that Caribbean humour translates globally when delivered with authenticity.

**Gil Croes (Aruba)** is one of the Caribbean's largest social media personalities, building a global audience through short-form entertaining content. His success on platforms like TikTok and Instagram shows how Caribbean personalities can compete in the global creator economy.

In 2016, SiliconCaribe organised the first Caribbean Bloggers Week, profiling Caribbean digital influencers across blogs, podcasts, Instagram, and YouTube. This event signalled the growing recognition that digital content creation is a legitimate career path in the region.

## Benefits and Limitations of Hosted Platforms

### Benefits

- **Low barrier to entry.** No server management, no coding, and often no cost.
- **Built-in audience.** Platforms like Medium and TikTok have existing user bases that can discover your content.
- **Managed infrastructure.** Hosting, security, and updates are handled for you.
- **Monetisation options.** Most platforms offer at least some path to earning revenue.

### Limitations

- **Limited customisation.** Free tiers restrict design, branding, and functionality.
- **Data and audience ownership.** On platforms like Medium, you do not own your audience.
- **Platform dependency.** If the platform changes its algorithm, terms of service, or shuts down, your content distribution channel disappears.
- **Branding limitations.** Free-tier plans often include watermarks, subdomains, and platform branding that undermine a professional image.

The fundamental trade-off is between convenience and control. Hosted platforms make publishing easy, but they also make you dependent on someone else's rules.

## Deployment Considerations for Caribbean Creators

Caribbean creators face unique challenges that affect platform choice:

- **High data costs.** Video-heavy content is expensive for both creators and audiences to access. A platform that requires constant high-resolution streaming may not serve a Caribbean audience struggling with data budgets.
- **Mobile-first audience.** Approximately 78% of Caribbean internet users access content primarily through smartphones. Your content must work well on mobile screens with varying connection speeds.
- **Loading times.** Bandwidth-constrained environments mean content that takes long to load gets abandoned. Lightweight platforms and optimised media perform better.
- **Cross-platform sharing.** Content must be shareable across WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook — the dominant social channels in the region. A blog post that cannot be easily shared on WhatsApp reaches fewer people.
- **Social commerce.** Forty percent of Caribbean online purchases originate from social media. Platforms that integrate with shopping features (Instagram Shopping, WhatsApp Business) offer creators additional revenue streams.

**Worked example:** A student in Jamaica wants to start a food blog showcasing local recipes. WordPress.com is a strong choice because it loads quickly, works on mobile, and can be integrated with Instagram for visual promotion. The student writes each post with lightweight images (optimised for mobile data), includes shareable links for WhatsApp, and links to a WhatsApp Business account for direct orders. The blog does not need heavy video — instead, it uses short Instagram Reels linked back to the written recipes on the blog.

## Age-Appropriate Considerations for Form 6 Students

- **You can start publishing immediately.** Free platforms like Blogger, Medium, and WordPress.com require no financial investment and are ideal for classroom exercises and personal portfolios.
- **Understanding monetisation models helps you plan.** Knowing how YouTube pays versus how Substack pays versus how Medium pays helps you choose a platform that matches your goals — even if those goals are years away.
- **Read the terms of service.** Every platform has rules about content ownership, data privacy, and age requirements. Substack, for example, requires users to be 18 for paid features. Understanding these terms is part of being a responsible digital citizen.
- **Study Caribbean creators.** Caribbean Pot, Chef Ricardo, and others show how cultural identity becomes a competitive advantage. You do not need to imitate global creators — your Caribbean perspective is your differentiator.
- **E-book publishing teaches real workflows.** Publishing through KDP or Smashwords gives you a tangible product and teaches you about formatting, cover design, pricing, and distribution — skills that transfer to professional contexts.

## Common Misconceptions

**"WordPress.com and WordPress.org are the same."**
They are not. WordPress.com is a hosted service where WordPress manages your site. WordPress.org is self-hosted software you install on your own server. Monetisation options, customisation, and control differ significantly between the two.

**"TikTok pays well for viral videos."**
Viral reach does not equal revenue. TikTok's Creator Rewards Programme pays significantly less per view than YouTube, and only a small percentage of creators see consistent returns. Most TikTok income comes from brand deals, not platform payments.

**"You need a large audience to publish online."**
Platforms like Medium and Substack provide built-in discovery. Even without a following, quality content can reach readers through platform algorithms and curation. The key is consistency and relevance, not follower count.

**"KDP exclusivity is forever."**
KDP Select requires 90-day exclusivity periods. After each period, you can remove your book and publish widely. You are locked in for three months at a time, not permanently.

**"Smashwords is defunct after merging with Draft2Digital."**
Smashwords continues operating as a distinct brand with its own catalogue and distribution relationships under Draft2Digital ownership. The merger expanded its reach rather than ending it.

## Summary and Key Takeaways

1. **Hosted platforms lower the barrier to publishing** but come with trade-offs in control, customisation, and audience ownership.
2. **Writing platforms serve different goals:** Medium for visibility, Substack for community monetisation, WordPress for brand ownership, Blogger for zero-cost experimentation.
3. **YouTube and TikTok serve different creator strategies:** YouTube rewards long-term audience building; TikTok rewards trend-driven virality.
4. **E-book self-publishing through KDP and Smashwords/D2D** gives Caribbean creators access to global markets without a traditional publishing deal.
5. **Caribbean creators face specific deployment challenges** — data costs, mobile-first audiences, and bandwidth constraints — that should inform platform choice.
6. **Cultural identity is a content strategy.** Caribbean creators like Caribbean Pot and Chef Ricardo prove that regional authenticity attracts global audiences.
7. **Platform dependency is the central risk.** Understanding terms of service, data ownership, and audience portability is essential for any serious creator.

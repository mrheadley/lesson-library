# Traditional Web CMS: WordPress, Drupal, Joomla

## Three Open-Source Giants, Three Different Philosophies

WordPress, Drupal, and Joomla are the three dominant open-source Content Management Systems. All three are free to download under the **GPLv2 licence**. All three power millions of websites. But they prioritise different things — and understanding those priorities is the key to choosing the right one.

**WordPress** prioritises ease of use and ecosystem breadth. **Drupal** prioritises content architecture, security, and scalability. **Joomla** occupies a middle ground between WordPress simplicity and Drupal complexity.

## WordPress: The Compound Ecosystem Advantage

WordPress powers **43% of all websites globally** and holds over 60% of the CMS market share. This dominance is not accidental — it is a compound advantage. More users attract more developers, more tutorials, more plugins, more themes, and cheaper support. Each new resource makes WordPress more attractive, which attracts more users. The lead grows over time.

**Core facts:**

- 60,000+ plugins and 12,000+ themes available
- Can be learned in **2–5 hours** to launch a basic site
- Uses the **Gutenberg block editor** (introduced in version 5.0), where content is composed of individual blocks — paragraphs, images, headings, galleries, and embeds
- WordPress with **WooCommerce** is the leading e-commerce CMS combination
- 5-year total cost of ownership: **$600–$3,000** (hosting, premium themes, plugins)

A school newspaper website is best built on WordPress because it is quick to set up, easy for student editors, and has abundant tutorials. WordPress with WooCommerce can power an online store for school fundraising with minimal technical knowledge.

### The Plugin Ecosystem

WordPress's 60,000+ plugins extend functionality without custom development:

- **Yoast SEO / RankMath**: Content analysis and technical SEO recommendations
- **WooCommerce**: E-commerce with product management, payments, and shipping
- **Contact Form 7 / WPForms**: Contact form creation
- **Elementor**: Visual page builder with drag-and-drop design

**But more plugins are not always better.** Too many plugins increase security risk, slow page load times, and create maintenance burden. Each plugin is a potential attack vector and a potential compatibility conflict. Install only what you need, keep everything updated, and remove unused plugins.

### Security

WordPress core is secure. The problem is the plugin ecosystem — **95% of hacked CMS sites in 2025 were WordPress** (Sucuri). This statistic is misleading without context: WordPress is hacked more often because it is used more often, and because its open plugin ecosystem invites poorly coded third-party additions. Properly maintained WordPress sites with vetted plugins are secure. The key is discipline: update regularly, use reputable plugins, and implement strong passwords.

## Drupal: Architecture and Security First

Drupal commands approximately **1.2% market share** — a fraction of WordPress. But it powers sites where content architecture, security, and scalability are non-negotiable: the **White House, NASA, and major government portals**.

**Core facts:**

- 50,000+ modules (the Drupal equivalent of plugins)
- **1–2 weeks** to learn basics (vs. hours for WordPress)
- Strongest **content modelling capabilities** of the three — native structured content types with complex field relationships
- Native **multilingual support** without requiring additional modules
- 5-year total cost of ownership: **$10,000–$100,000+**
- Dedicated **proactive security team** — Drupal's complex structure also deters mass automated attacks

A university course catalogue with complex relationships between departments, courses, and instructors benefits from Drupal's content modelling. A Caribbean government ministry portal requiring multiple languages and strict approval workflows suits Drupal's capabilities.

### The Learning Curve

Drupal's steep learning curve is its greatest barrier. Where WordPress lets a beginner publish a post in minutes, Drupal requires understanding content types, views, permissions, and module configuration before anything appears on screen. A student choosing Drupal for a personal project should expect to invest **several weeks** rather than an afternoon.

### Why Drupal When WordPress Exists

Drupal is justified when you need:

- Complex content structures with many relationships
- Enterprise-grade permissions and editorial workflows
- Native multilingual support
- Highest security standards
- Scalability for high-traffic institutional sites

If none of these apply, WordPress is almost certainly the better choice.

## Joomla: The Middle Ground

Joomla holds approximately **1.6% market share** and occupies the space between WordPress and Drupal. It offers:

- **Built-in multilingual support** without plugins
- Strong **Access Control Lists (ACL)** for granular permission management
- Approximately **6,000 extensions**
- 5-year total cost of ownership: **$1,500–$5,000**

A community centre website with membership features and event management could work on Joomla's built-in access controls.

### The Declining Community

Joomla's community has been **shrinking since 2020**. Fewer developers, fewer tutorials, fewer new extensions. Long-term viability is a consideration — if you invest in a Joomla site today, the ecosystem supporting it may be smaller in five years. This does not make Joomla broken; it makes it a riskier long-term bet.

## The General Recommendation

Use **WordPress** unless you have a specific structural reason for Drupal or Joomla. WordPress's ecosystem advantage compounds over time — more developers, cheaper support, more tutorials, more plugins. The WordPress community is the largest in the CMS world, and that community is a resource you draw on every time you hit a problem.

Choose **Drupal** if you need complex content architecture, enterprise governance, native multilingual, or the highest security standards — and you have the development resources to support it.

Choose **Joomla** if you need built-in multilingual and strong ACL without Drupal's complexity, and you accept the shrinking community risk.

## Open Source Is Not Free

All three are open-source under GPLv2 — free to download, free to modify, free to distribute. But the software is free in the same way the air is free. **Hosting costs money.** Premium themes cost money. Plugins cost money. Developer time costs money. The total cost of ownership includes hosting, premium extensions, customisation, and ongoing maintenance.

A WordPress site on basic hosting with a premium theme and essential plugins costs $600–$3,000 over five years. A Drupal site with custom development can exceed $100,000. The software is free; the operation is not.

## Common Misconceptions

### "WordPress is only for blogs"
WordPress powers blogs, business sites, e-commerce stores, membership sites, portfolios, and complex web applications. The blog-first reputation is outdated by a decade.

### "Drupal is always more secure than WordPress"
Drupal's architecture is more secure by design, but properly maintained WordPress sites with vetted plugins are also secure. The statistic about WordPress hacks reflects its dominance, not inherent insecurity.

### "Joomla is outdated and irrelevant"
Joomla remains functional for specific use cases — membership sites, multilingual portals, community platforms. It is not outdated; its community is shrinking, which affects long-term support.

### "Open-source means free"
The software is free. Hosting, premium themes, plugins, and developer time create real costs. Budget for the total cost of ownership, not just the download.

### "More plugins mean a better site"
Too many plugins increase security risk, slow page loads, and create maintenance burden. Install only what you need. A site with 5 well-chosen plugins outperforms a site with 50 random ones.

### "The most popular CMS is always the best choice"
WordPress is best for most cases, but Drupal or Joomla may be better for specific requirements like enterprise governance or complex content structures. Match the tool to the problem.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Monolithic CMS | Architecture where content management, database, and presentation are tightly coupled |
| Gutenberg Editor | WordPress's block-based content editor using modular content blocks |
| ACL | Access Control List — Joomla's granular permission management system |
| Plugin Ecosystem | The collection of third-party add-ons extending CMS functionality |
| Module (Drupal) | Drupal's equivalent of a WordPress plugin |
| Content Modelling | Defining structured content types with specific fields and relationships |
| GPLv2 | Open-source licence allowing free use and modification of the software |
| Total Cost of Ownership | Complete cost of operating a system over a defined period |

## Summary

WordPress, Drupal, and Joomla are three open-source CMS platforms with different priorities. WordPress dominates with 43% market share, compound ecosystem advantage, and the lowest barrier to entry. Drupal offers enterprise-grade content modelling, security, and scalability at the cost of a steep learning curve and high TCO. Joomla occupies the middle ground with built-in multilingual and strong ACL, but faces a shrinking community. The general recommendation is WordPress unless you have a specific reason for Drupal or Joomla. Remember: open-source software is free, but the ecosystem around it — hosting, themes, plugins, and developer time — is not.

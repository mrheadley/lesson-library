# Publishing Workshop: Choosing a Platform

## From Theory to Decision

Previous lessons gave you the knowledge — e-book formats, podcast production, security practices, evaluation criteria. This workshop is where you apply that knowledge to make a real platform choice for a real brief. The article serves as your decision-making reference.

The platform selection matrix evaluates options across five dimensions: **content type, audience, budget, skills, and timeline.** These are the non-negotiable factors in any publishing decision.

## The Platform Selection Matrix

Before looking at any platform, answer these questions about your project:

1. **Content type** — What are you creating? Text, audio, video, images, or a combination?
2. **Audience** — Who will consume this? What devices do they use? When and where do they engage with content?
3. **Budget** — What money is available? If zero, what free tools exist?
4. **Skills** — What can the team actually do right now, not what they hope to learn?
5. **Timeline** — When must it be live? Tomorrow? Next month? This semester?

These five questions narrow the field before you compare a single platform feature.

## Free Platform Options

For projects with no budget, these are the practical choices:

**Blogger** — Google-owned, sign in with a Google account, live in minutes. Best for: simple personal blogs, class assignments, low-complexity projects. Limitations: minimal design options, limited customisation, Google controls the platform.

**WordPress.com free tier** — more structured than Blogger with categories, pages, and a block editor. Best for: structured blogs, school news, project documentation. Limitations: WordPress branding on your site, limited themes, no plugin installation, no custom domain.

**Medium** — built-in audience of millions of readers. Best for: opinion pieces, essays, and content that benefits from existing readership. Limitations: no custom domain on free tier, no email list building, Medium controls the algorithm, content lives on medium.com.

**Spotify for Podcasters** (formerly Anchor) — free hosting and distribution to all major directories. Best for: starting a podcast with zero budget. Limitations: limited analytics, Spotify-branded, no advanced editing features.

**YouTube** — massive reach, built-in audience, free hosting. Best for: video content, tutorials, visual demonstrations. Limitations: algorithm-dependent discovery, copyright strikes can remove content, monetisation requirements are steep.

## Paid Platform Options

When budget is available, these offer greater control and professionalism:

**WordPress.org + hosting** ($5–30/month) — full control, 60,000+ plugins, unlimited customisation. The industry standard for serious publishing. Requires initial setup time — installing the CMS, choosing a theme, configuring plugins. But once running, it's the most flexible option available.

**Squarespace** ($16–49/month) — beautiful templates, drag-and-drop editing, good for portfolios and visual projects. No coding required. Limitations: less flexible than WordPress, you're on their infrastructure.

**Ghost** ($9–25/month) — purpose-built for publishing and newsletters. Clean, fast, focused on writers. Good for subscription-based content.

|----------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| Blogger | Minutes | None |
| WordPress.com | Minutes to hours | None to basic |
| Medium | Minutes | None |

If your deadline is tight, the top four options can have content live today. WordPress.org and Squarespace require more upfront investment but deliver better long-term results.

## Decision Shortcuts

**No budget, simple blog:** Blogger — zero cost, live in minutes, minimal learning curve.

**No budget, structured content:** WordPress.com free tier — categories, pages, better organisation than Blogger.

**Opinion pieces with built-in audience:** Medium — the audience is already there, but you don't control the platform.

**Podcast, zero budget:** Spotify for Podcasters — free hosting, free distribution, minimal friction.

**Portfolio site, some budget:** Squarespace — polished templates that showcase visual work without coding.

**Portfolio site, no budget, technically inclined:** GitHub Pages — free, version-controlled, requires HTML/CSS knowledge.

**Serious long-term publishing:** WordPress.org with hosting — full ownership, full control, the standard for professional publishing.

## Owning Your Domain

A domain name (yoursite.com) is portable — you can point it to any host at any time. A platform subdomain (yoursite.wordpress.com, yoursite.blogspot.com) is tied to that platform and signals that you're on a free tier.

A custom domain costs approximately $12 per year through registrars like Cloudflare or Namecheap. This is the single most important long-term investment in your digital presence. Even if you start on a free platform today, own your domain from the beginning.

### Worked Example: Domain Portability

1. Purchase yoursite.com on Cloudflare for $12/year.
2. Start on WordPress.com free tier. Point your domain to WordPress.com.
3. After six months, outgrow WordPress.com's limitations.
4. Purchase hosting and install WordPress.org. Point your domain to the new host.
5. Your URL doesn't change. Search engine rankings are preserved. Visitors don't notice the switch.

This is domain portability in action — your identity travels with you because you own it.

## Content Export: Verify Before You Commit

Before committing to any platform, verify that you can export your content. WordPress, Squarespace, and Blogger all offer export functions that produce portable XML or ZIP files. Some platforms make this deliberately difficult.

Test the export: create a sample post, then export it. Can you import it somewhere else? If the answer is no or uncertain, reconsider the platform.

## Walled Gardens

A **walled garden** is a closed ecosystem where the platform operator controls content, applications, and services, making it difficult or impossible for users to move outside. The longer you stay, the harder it becomes to leave — this is by design.

Examples of walled garden behaviour: no content export function, proprietary file formats that don't translate elsewhere, analytics that can't be downloaded, audience data that can't be exported.

Free platforms tend to be more walled than paid ones, but not always. Evaluate each platform on its specific export capabilities, not on assumptions.

Compare the real cost over one year:

| Platform | Monthly Cost | Annual Cost | Domain | Total Year 1 |

| Blogger | $0 | $0 | $12 (optional) | $0–12 |

The cheapest option is not always the best value. A $0 platform with no export and no custom domain may cost you more in lost flexibility than a $12/month option that you fully control.

## Common Misconceptions

### "The most expensive platform is always the best"
The best platform is the one that matches your specific needs. A free Blogger site may outperform a $49/month Squarespace site if it's well-written and serves its audience.

### "You need to make the perfect choice immediately"
Starting on a free platform to test your concept, then migrating to a paid option as you grow, is a perfectly valid and common approach. Perfection is not required on day one.

### "Once you choose a platform, you're stuck with it forever"
Most platforms allow content export. The key is to verify export capability before committing and to always own your domain name.

### "More features means a better platform"
Features you don't need add complexity and cost. Choose the simplest platform that meets your actual requirements, not hypothetical future needs.

### "A student project doesn't need a real domain name"
A custom domain signals professionalism, is portable, and costs only $12/year. It's the most valuable long-term investment in your digital presence.

### "Free platforms are unprofessional"
Many successful publishers started on free platforms. Content quality matters more than platform tier. You can always upgrade later while keeping your domain.

## Key Terms

## Summary

Platform selection is a structured decision, not a guess. Use the matrix — content type, audience, budget, skills, timeline — to narrow options before comparing features. Free platforms (Blogger, WordPress.com, Medium) are valid starting points; paid platforms (WordPress.org, Squarespace) offer greater control and ownership. Purchase your domain name independently for portability. Verify content export before committing. The best platform is the simplest one that meets your actual requirements today, with a clear path to upgrade when you outgrow it.

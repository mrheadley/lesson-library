# Guerrilla Marketing: Outsmarting, Not Outspending

## What Guerrilla Marketing Is (and Isn't)

**Guerrilla marketing** is unconventional, low-cost, high-impact promotion that relies on creativity and surprise rather than a big ad budget. The core idea: a small operator can out-earn a big competitor by being clever, timely, and location-relevant — the marketing equivalent of a chess player winning with fewer pieces through a better opening.

Keep the contrast clear, because it anchors everything:

- **Guerrilla** (this lesson): unconventional, cheap, often one-off — viral stunts, ambient art, street interventions.
- **Influencer/paid** (next lesson): scaled, recurring, and *paid*.
- **Email/owned** (the lesson after): recurring and *owned*.

Guerrilla is best applied at **launch or around big events**, where one memorable stunt can convert directly into downloads and earned media — not as an ongoing strategy.

## The Five Ingredients of a Working Stunt

1. **Know exactly who you're surprising.** A stunt that surprises "everyone" surprises no one meaningfully; it has to hit the *right* people where they are.
2. **Keep the mechanic dead simple.** If it takes more than a sentence to explain, it will not spread.
3. **Make it inherently shareable / photographable.** The goal is selfies, screenshots, and shares — the *audience* becomes the distribution channel.
4. **Link it to a clear action.** Install, visit, buy — a famous stunt with no call-to-action is fame without business.
5. **Keep it on-concept.** The best stunts reinforce the product's *actual value* — a food app pulling a stunt about food, a weather app stunting on a storm day. A random prank is attention without relevance.

## The Classic Cases

Three cases keep appearing because each teaches a different thing:

- **ALS Ice Bucket Challenge** (2014): essentially zero media spend, **~$115M raised in 8 weeks**, **17M+ videos**, 10B+ views. The lesson: a dead-simple mechanic with built-in social pressure and shareability turns participants into the distribution channel.
- **Dollar Shave Club**: a founder-authentic launch video on roughly **$4,500**, about **$0.37 per acquired customer**, ~12,000 initial subscribers, later a **$1B** acquisition. The lesson: authenticity and shareability can beat a big media budget.
- **Domino's "Paving for Pizza"**: fixing potholes near bad roads, tagged with a pizza-box lid — a public-good stunt that is *geographically relevant* and earns media at low cost. The lesson: relevance and a feel-good hook travel far.

## The Trade-Off You Must Weigh

Guerrilla is cheap per stunt but **not reliably repeatable** — it is stunts, not scalable media. The honest trade-off:

- **Low cost:** an ambient stunt can cost a fraction of equivalent paid reach.
- **Not repeatable:** you cannot run the same stunt every day; it works because it is a surprise.
- **Risk of backfire:** a tasteless, illegal, or misjudged stunt produces *negative* earned media. Attention is not automatically good attention.

So guerrilla is a **launch/buzz tool**, not a stay-at-home strategy — and every idea needs a quick ethics/legality/taste audit before it runs.

## Worked Example: A Local Launch Stunt

A school/community news app wants launch buzz. A guerrilla idea:

- **Who:** students and residents on one campus at a popular spot.
- **Mechanic:** a single, simple, photo-worthy moment tied to "stay informed fast" — e.g. a striking temporary display of fast-updating local headlines.
- **Shareable:** designed so anyone with a phone can capture and share it; a tiny QR/URL (or a data-light way to reach the app) is the clear call-to-action.
- **On-concept:** it is *about news/alerts*, not a random dance.
- **Risk audit:** fully permissioned space, culturally appropriate, nothing offensive — so it cannot backfire.

That is a cheap, on-concept, self-spreading launch moment — classic guerrilla, applied locally.

## Caribbean Applications

Guerrilla fits the region well because **large paid budgets are rarely possible but social penetration is high**. Culturally specific, low-cost tactics — carnival tie-ins, local landmarks, school and community events, and WhatsApp-forward sharing — can outperform a generic global campaign. The regional data (WhatsApp above 85%, strong social penetration) means a self-spreading local stunt can travel fast through existing sharing habits.

## Common Misconceptions

### "Guerrilla marketing is another word for social media posts."
No — it is specifically *unconventional, often offline/one-off* tactics (stunts, ambient, viral mechanics). Recurring social/paid content is a different, scalable channel.

### "It's free, so the more stunts the better."
Each stunt carries attention *and* risk. A flood of weak stunts dilutes the brand and raises the odds of a backfire — quality and on-concept coherence beat quantity.

### "Any viral stunt will sell the product."
A stunt that does not connect to the app's value (the on-concept test) or give a clear call-to-action generates fame, not installs.

### "Big brands can't be guerrilla."
Small operators often beat big brands here, because the weapon is creativity and timing, not budget. It is the underdog's tool.

### "Get noticed at all costs."
The backfire case proves attention is not inherently good — negative earned media can outweigh the gain. Audit ethics/legality/taste first.

### "Guerrilla replaces real marketing."
It is a launch/buzz tool, not a scalable strategy. It works *alongside* sustained owned/paid/earned channels, not instead of them.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Guerrilla marketing | Unconventional, low-cost tactics using creativity, surprise, and location/timing to generate attention |
| Ambient marketing | Embedding the message in the environment (subway, street, buildings) |
| On-concept stunt | A stunt that reinforces the product's actual value, not a random attention-getter |
| Viral mechanic | The built-in share loop (photo-ability, "challenge three friends") that makes the audience the channel |
| Earned media | Coverage/shares the stunt wins on merit rather than pays for |
| Backfire risk | When a tasteless/illegal/misjudged stunt generates negative attention instead of positive buzz |

## Summary

Guerrilla marketing outsmarts, not outspends: unconventional, low-cost, surprise-driven tactics aimed at a precise audience, with a simple mechanic, shareable by design, tied to a clear action, and kept on-concept. The classic cases — Ice Bucket, Dollar Shave Club, Paving for Pizza — each teach one ingredient. Weigh the low cost against the lack of repeatability and the backfire risk, and always audit ethics, legality, and taste before you run anything. Guerrilla is the launch-buzz tool of the underdog, and it fits the Caribbean's low-budget, high-sharing reality particularly well.

# Influencer Marketing: Borrowed Trust, Priced Accordingly

## The Idea Behind the Practice

**Influencer marketing** uses individuals with engaged audiences — from nano to mega — to promote an app, trading on the trust and reach they have built with their followers. The mechanism is **borrowed trust**: a follower trusts the creator, so that trust transfers (positively or negatively) to the app they promote. If the creator's audience is wrong, the money is wasted; if the creator is untrustworthy, the association hurts you.

This is a **rented, personality-driven channel** — you pay for the access, and you do not own the audience. Keep that contrast with owned email (last lesson) and guerrilla (the one before): influencer marketing is *scaled, recurring, and paid*; guerrilla is *one-off and cheap*; email is *owned and recurring*.

## The Tier Ladder: Reach vs Authenticity

Creators are usually grouped by follower count, and each tier trades reach against authenticity, cost, and engagement:

| Tier | Size | Typical strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Nano** | 1K–10K | Highest relative authenticity and engagement; very cost-efficient for niche apps |
| **Micro** | 10K–100K | A strong default: engaged, authentic, affordable |
| **Macro** | 100K–1M | Wider reach, higher cost, more diluted per-follower trust |
| **Mega / celebrity** | 1M+ | Massive awareness and credibility-by-association, but expensive and lower per-follower engagement |

The steady finding is that **micro and nano creators often deliver higher engagement and more authentic, cost-efficient influence** than macro/mega for a niche app launch. Mega influencers buy impressiveness and scale — at a premium, and with the risk that excess reach dilutes relevance.

## The Success Factors (What Actually Predicts Results)

- **Audience fit (relevance), not follower count.** Does the creator's *actual audience* match the app's target users? This outweighs raw follower numbers every time.
- **Authentic integration.** The app shown in context, naturally — not a scripted, logo-stuffed ad. Forced placements feel inauthentic and underperform.
- **Clear measurement.** Track which installs/signups actually came from which creator, and judge them on that.

A useful teaching data point: targeted influencer campaigns can lower cost-per-install by roughly **35%** versus broad paid — but only when the audience is *relevant*. An influencer without the right audience can burn the budget for zero installs.

## The Attribution Caution (Don't Over-Credit the Last Click)

An influencer post is often **not the last touchpoint**. A real install journey might be: TikTok post → YouTube video → store search → install. If you judge the influencer only on the final click, you *undercount* their contribution; if you credit them with everything, you *overcount* it. Modern practice uses **multi-touch attribution** — treating the influencer as one contributor among several — rather than handing one channel the whole credit.

## Ethics, Disclosure, and Fraud

This is where the course's ethics thread and "accountability as representation" really apply:

- **Disclosure:** sponsored content must be clearly marked (paid partnership, `#ad`). Undisclosed promotion is a trust-breaker and increasingly regulated.
- **Fake metrics:** inflated/fake followers are widespread; vetted engagement matters more than a big number. Buying a creator with fake followers is a fraud risk.
- **Over-reliance:** resting your launch on one personality concentrates risk — if that creator's reputation tanks, the association hurts you too.

Being honest about the relationship is both the ethical choice and the commercially safer one.

## Worked Example: Which Creator Do You Pick?

A niche app for students (study/scheduling). You have two offers:

- **Nano creator:** 5,000 genuine followers, almost all students, high comments-to-views ratio, cheap.
- **Mega celebrity:** 5 million followers, mostly not students, expensive, but huge.

The influencer lesson says: **fit beats count.** The nano creator's audience matches the app's target; the mega celebrity's does not — so the nano is the better choice *for this app*, for engagement and cost. The mega might buy impressiveness, but students scroll past content that is not for them.

## Regional Application: Local Creators for Local Apps

For a Caribbean app launch, **local micro influencers — community figures, carnival/culture creators, school/university scenes — can outperform imported big names.** Local audiences are smaller, and social penetration is high, so a relevant local creator reaches the right people with credibility that a global celebrity cannot match. Relevance and fit beat volume in a smaller market just as they do in a big one.

## Common Misconceptions

### "The more followers, the better the influencer."
Audience *fit* and engagement beat raw follower count. Micro/nano creators often deliver more authentic, cost-efficient results for niche launches.

### "Mega influencers are the only way to go big."
Mega scale costs a premium and can dilute relevance. For most small/niche apps, the "wrong mega" wastes budget — scale should follow fit, not replace it.

### "An influencer post instantly means installs."
It earns attention and interest. Conversion depends on the message, the store page, and the whole journey — and the last click rarely belongs to the influencer.

### "All a post needs is the app's logo."
Forced, scripted placements underperform and feel inauthentic. In-context, disclosed, honest integration is what actually transfers trust.

### "Follower counts are trustworthy."
Inflated/fake followers are widespread. Engagement and audience genuinely need vetting; undisclosed/fake promotion is an ethical and regulatory risk.

### "One big influencer guarantees a launch."
Over-relying on one personality concentrates risk (reputation contagion). A mix across tiers and channels is more resilient.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Influencer / creator | An individual, of any audience size, who shapes their followers' attention, opinions, or behaviour |
| Nano / micro / macro / mega | The tier ladder by follower count, balancing reach, authenticity, cost, and engagement |
| Borrowed trust | Transferring the follower's trust in the creator (positively or negatively) to the app |
| Audience fit (relevance) | How well the creator's audience matches the app's target users — the real predictor |
| Multi-touch attribution | Crediting the influencer with the other touchpoints that contributed, not just the last click |
| Disclosure | Clearly labelling sponsored content so the audience knows the relationship |

## Summary

Influencer marketing is borrowed trust, priced accordingly: choose creators on **audience fit and authenticity, not follower count**, pick from the tier ladder, and measure actual installs per creator. Be aware of multi-touch attribution so you neither over- nor under-credit one channel, and run it with disclosure and vetted, real engagement to avoid both the ethical and the fraud traps. For a local app, relevant local micro creators often beat imported big names. It is a rented channel that works best integrated alongside the owned and guerrilla strategies around it.

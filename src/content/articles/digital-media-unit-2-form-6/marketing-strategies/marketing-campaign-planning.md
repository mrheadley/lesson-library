# Marketing Campaign Planning: From Wishlist to Plan

## What a Campaign Plan Is For

The promotion tricks you have learned — guerrilla, email, influencer — are only *tactics*. A **campaign plan** is the structure that turns those tactics into a coordinated, budgeted, measurable effort. It answers the practical questions: *who, what, where, when, how, how much, and how will we know it worked?*

A campaign is not "a set of posts." It is a **planned, time-boxed, coordinated effort with goals, audience, message, channels, timing, budget, and metrics** — with a measurement loop that tells you whether it worked so you can adjust.

## The Standard Plan Structure

The discipline of planning moves in a logical order. Work through it in sequence:

1. **Goals** — make them SMART: **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**chievable, **R**elevant, **T**ime-bound.
2. **Audience** — a persona and clear segments (who, exactly, are we talking to?).
3. **Message** — the single, consistent value claim across every channel.
4. **Channels** — which promotion tools from the recent lessons (guerrilla L148, owned/email L149, influencer/paid L150).
5. **Timing** — a calendar: what happens when.
6. **Budget** — how much, spread where.
7. **KPIs / metrics** — the numbers that tell you each objective is being met.
8. **Measurement & review** — the loop that lets you adjust.

## SMART Objectives: The Discipline That Makes Goals Usable

The single most common planning failure is a vague objective like "raise awareness." You cannot measure that, so you cannot know if you succeeded. The SMART discipline forces an objective into a testable, time-bound target.

> **Vague:** "Get lots of users."
> **SMART:** "£30 budget, 750 installs and ≥40% first-week activation by March 15."

Now you know what success looks like, and you can check against it. Every objective in a campaign should be phraseable this way.

## Budget: Owned, Guerrilla, and a Few Paid Plays

A small launch gets the strongest return by *diversifying* limited budget rather than dumping it on one channel:

- **Owned (email, content):** near-zero marginal cost — you already own the list (L149).
- **Guerrilla:** low-cost stunts (L148).
- **A few targeted paid/influencer plays:** where relevance is high (L150).

Diversification also lowers risk: if one channel underperforms, the plan is not sunk. The plan allocates across these, then *measures* to decide where future budget should go — rather than betting everything on one guess.

## Timing: The Campaign Calendar

Timing is planned, not improvised. A launch campaign is structured around a **calendar** with distinct phases:

- **Pre-launch buzz** — build a waitlist/community, tease, set up.
- **Launch-day burst** — the concentrated push to spike installs and visibility.
- **Post-launch steady state** — retention, content cadence, scaling what works.

Each channel is assigned to a phase. The calendar makes the "when" of every action explicit and keeps a chaotic, talkative class organised around concrete dates.

## Measurement: Setting Up Before You Launch

A plan without metrics is a wishlist; a plan that only adds measurement *after* launch is often unable to answer "did it work?" So part of the plan is **setting up tracking before you go live**:

- **UTM links** on every campaign URL (so you can see which channel drove an install).
- **Store analytics** (views, installs, conversion).
- **Web analytics** (visitor → capture-point conversion).

Map each objective to a metric and a tool *in the plan itself*: e.g. "objective = 750 installs → metric = store install count → tool = Play/App Store analytics."

## Worked Example: A One-Page Plan

Here is how the structure fits together for a small app:

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Goal (SMART)** | 500 installs, ≥35% activation, within 4 weeks, £40 budget |
| **Audience** | Local students aged 16–18 on campus |
| **Message** | "Study faster, offline" — one consistent claim |
| **Channels** | Owned email (existing), guerrilla campus stunt, one targeted local micro-influencer |
| **Timing** | Pre-launch: 1 week buzz → launch: 3-day burst → post: weekly content |
| **Budget** | £5 guerrilla + £30 influencer + £5 misc |
| **KPIs** | 500 installs, 35% activation, CTR on each channel |

That is a *plan* — every element is filled with a concrete decision, and each is measurable.

## Regional Realism

The plan must be written for the region's reality, not a global template. Caribbean constraints — data cost, smaller local reach, device floors (Lessons 106–119) — change **where and when**:

- Keep **low-data channels** (WhatsApp/SMS) prominent.
- Schedule bursts around **local events** (carnival, school terms) rather than generic dates.
- Weight channels by where local users actually are.

A plan refitted for regional reality outperforms a copy-pasted global one.

## Common Misconceptions

### "A campaign is just a set of posts."
A campaign has goals, audience, message, channels, timing, budget, and metrics — posts are execution of a plan, not the plan itself.

### "Goals like 'raise awareness' are fine."
Vague objectives can't be measured. The SMART discipline turns them into testable, time-bound targets.

### "Budget should go to whichever channel someone recommends."
A sound plan diversifies limited budget across owned (cheap), guerrilla (low-cost), and a few targeted paid plays, then measures to decide where more goes.

### "There's no 'when' — we just post continuously."
Timing is planned (pre-launch buzz, launch burst, post-launch steady state, event tie-ins). A calendar is part of the plan.

### "We'll figure out measurement later."
You must set up tracking (UTMs, analytics) *before* launch, or the "did it work?" question is unanswerable. Attribution set-up is budgeted into the plan.

### "One global plan works everywhere."
Caribbean constraints change the channel mix and timing. The plan must be written for regional reality.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Campaign | A coordinated, time-boxed marketing effort with goals, channels, budget, and measurement |
| SMART objective | Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound — turning aims into testable targets |
| KPIs / metrics | Concrete numbers (reach, CTR, installs, signups, activations, revenue) for each objective |
| Macro → micro planning | Starting with the big picture before specifying individual campaign details |
| Campaign calendar | The timeline mapping every action to a channel and phase |
| Attribution set-up | Pre-configuring tracking (UTM, analytics, store metrics) so results are measurable |

## Summary

A campaign plan is the structure that turns marketing tactics into a coordinated, measurable effort. Work through it in order — SMART goals, audience, message, channels, timing, budget, KPIs, review — and make every objective concrete enough to test. Diversify limited budget across owned, guerrilla, and a few targeted paid plays, plan your timing around pre/launch/post phases, and set up your tracking *before* you go live so the "did it work?" question is answerable. Finally, refit the plan to the region's data and platform reality, not a global template.

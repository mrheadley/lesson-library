# Global Mobile Infrastructure & Statistics: The World's Baseline

## The Ladder of Numbers That Nobody Reads Correctly

Pick up a headline: "112 mobile subscriptions per 100 people worldwide" or "5G covers 55% of the Earth." Both are true — and both are routinely misread, because "connectivity" is a ladder and the numbers shrink on every rung. The rungs, in order:

1. Mobile-cellular **subscriptions** (SIM cards sold) — highest count.
2. Unique mobile **subscribers** (actual people with a phone) — lower, because people carry several SIMs.
3. **Smartphone** adoption — how many of those phones are smartphones.
4. Mobile **internet users** — people who actually go online on their own device — lowest.

Every rung below the first deflates the one above. Learn to ask *which rung* a statistic measures before quoting it.

## Subscriptions vs. Subscribers: A Subscription Is Not a Person

The single most important distinction in mobile statistics is between a **subscription** and a **subscriber**:

- **Mobile-cellular subscription** — a SIM card sold and active, whether on a pay-as-you-go or contract plan. One person can hold several.
- **Unique mobile subscriber** — a distinct human being who uses a mobile service, as estimated by GSMA Intelligence.

The ITU counted about **9.2 billion subscriptions in 2025** — **112 per 100 inhabitants globally** (142 per 100 in high-income countries, 70 per 100 in low-income ones). But GSMA Intelligence puts **unique subscribers at about 5.83 billion in April 2026, roughly 70% of the world's population**, growing about 1.8% a year (+103 million annually). That is a gap of over three billion — every one of those is a SIM card, not an extra person.

The Caribbean makes the point perfectly. Duopoly islands like Jamaica run penetration commonly above 100% (about 104–112%) because of prepaid top-ups and multi-SIM habits. When students report "everyone has a phone because penetration is 112%," they are confusing a sold SIM with a person. **Penetration exceeds 100% because there are more subscriptions than inhabitants.**

## Smartphone Adoption: The Middle Rung

Once people subscribe, the next question is what is in their hand. Ericsson data (relayed by DataReportal's Digital 2026 Mid-Year Update) shows smartphones are about **89% of mobile phones in use — around 7.6 billion handsets — or roughly 85.5% of cellular connections**. The remainder are feature phones: basic devices that lean on voice and USSD, unable to run modern apps.

"89% smartphone share" is a global average pulled by a few vast markets. Emerging regions replace feature phones more slowly, so their share drags the number down — and they are where the next billion smartphone users come from.

## Coverage: The 4G and 5G Rollout

Coverage measures *where a signal exists*, not *who uses it* — a subtle but crucial difference. The rollout is nearly complete for recent generations and only beginning for 5G:

- **4G covers about 93% of the global population** (roughly 7.6 billion people) — GSMA's State of Mobile Internet Connectivity 2025, with ITU broadly in agreement.
- **5G covers about 54–55% of the world's population** (roughly 4.4 billion people) — ITU says 55%. It reaches **~84% of people in high-income countries but only ~4% in low-income countries**.
- **5G subscriptions** hit about **2.9 billion by end-2025** (about one-third of all subscriptions, past 3 billion during 2025 — Ericsson), or about **36% of mobile-broadband subscriptions** (ITU). Ericsson expects 5G to overtake 4G as the dominant technology around **end-2027**.

Ninety-three percent 4G coverage sounds like "nearly done." It is not the same as "fast everywhere." Next comes the gap that actually matters.

## The Coverage Gap vs. the Usage Gap

This is the most underrated statistic in the industry. The **coverage gap** is the share of humanity with no signal at all. The **usage gap** is the share who live inside a signal but do not subscribe to or use mobile internet.

GSMA's numbers for 2025:

| Layer | Who | Share of population |
|-------|-----|---------------------|
| Living in a mobile-broadband footprint | ~96% | nearly everyone |
| Using mobile internet on their own device | ~4.7 billion | ~58% |
| In the **coverage gap** (no signal at all) | ~300 million | ~4% |
| In the **usage gap** (signal exists, not used) | ~3.1 billion | ~38% |

Let the scale sink in: **about 9 in 10 people not using mobile internet still live where a signal exists.** The barrier is not coverage — it is device cost and data affordability. When a map shows "96% coverage," the honest next sentence is "but only a little over half of humanity can afford to use it."

## What Sets the Regions Apart

Regional averages are not a uniform ladder — they are a spread across the same rollout curve. And two sources disagree, which is itself a lesson.

**Regional 5G population coverage (ITU, 2025):** Europe ~74% · Asia-Pacific ~70% · Americas ~60% · Arab States ~13% · Africa ~12% · CIS ~8%.

**Regional 5G population coverage (Ericsson, end-2025):** Africa ~10% · Latin America ~30% · Middle East ~25% · Asia-Pacific (ex-China/India) ~35% · North America/China/India ~90–95%.

Notice where they clash: ITU says the Americas are ~60% covered, Ericsson gives Latin America only ~30%; Africa is ~12% (ITU) versus ~10% (Ericsson); Europe is ~74% (ITU) versus roughly ~85% (Ericsson). The firms define the "covered" boundary differently, and neither silently "wins." **The rule: define the metric and name the source before quoting the number.**

On subscriptions, Ericsson's end-2025 view:

| Region | 5G subscription penetration |
|--------|-----------------------------|
| North America | ~79% |
| North East Asia | ~61% |
| Western Europe | ~55% |
| GCC | ~55% |
| India / Nepal / Bhutan | ~394M subs (~32%) |
| Sub-Saharan Africa | ~30M subs |

A couple of big countries move the global average almost by themselves: India drove more than half of the world's *new* 5G-covered population in 2024 and reaches ~80% of its people, while China alone held ~1,010 million of the end-2024 5G subscriptions in North East Asia. Sub-Saharan Africa, by contrast, grew 5G subscriptions at the world's highest *rate* (roughly 54% CAGR to 2031) from a tiny base — for every 100 subscriptions there, only about 5 are on 5G.

## Where the Caribbean Sits

The Caribbean and Latin America are **not** in the "developed, saturated" column. The region's profile is: 4G dominated (about **72% of subscriptions were still on 4G at end-2024**), 5G arriving late (about **60–63 million 5G subscriptions at end-2024**, heading toward an estimated **480–553 million by 2030–31**), and regional 5G **coverage around just 30% in 2025** — Ericsson figures. On an island like Jamaica, 5G only began rolling out in earnest from 2024. The honest frame is an **emerging-market profile**: high multi-SIM penetration, late 5G, large affordability-driven usage gap.

## Reading the Numbers Critically: Flags

- **"5 billion 5G subscriptions" does not mean 5 billion people on 5G.** Subscriptions count SIMs; unique 5G *users* are far fewer.
- **"'112% penetration' does not mean everyone has a phone.** Many people hold two or more SIMs, especially in dual-SIM Caribbean and Asian markets.
- **"96% coverage" does not mean everyone is online.** The usage gap (~38% of the world) is nearly ten times the coverage gap (~4%).
- **"Unique mobile users" ≠ "internet users."** GSMA's ~5.8 billion is about phones; ITU's ~6.0 billion online (~74% of the world) is about *internet access* — different methods, different definitions.
- **Analytics firms disagree, and that is normal.** ITU and Ericsson are both credible, measuring "5G coverage" slightly differently (see the regional table). Always state source and year.

## Common Misconceptions

### "1 billion 5G subscriptions means 1 billion people are on 5G"
Subscriptions count SIMs, and connection-based counts from firms like Ericsson are not people. Unique users are always lower — sometimes by a wide margin.

### "112% penetration means everyone has a phone"
Penetration divides subscriptions by population. Many people hold two or more SIMs; the count inflates against headcount.

### "Coverage of 96% means almost everyone is online"
Only a little over half the world actually uses mobile internet. The usage gap (38%) dwarfs the coverage gap (4%).

### "One graph, one truth — all firms agree"
They do not. ITU and Ericsson give different regional 5G coverage figures (e.g. Africa ~12% vs. ~10%, Americas ~60% vs. Latin America ~30%). Define the metric and source first.

### "The Caribbean belongs in the developed, saturated column"
It sits closer to Latin America's emerging profile: 4G-dominated, late 5G, high multi-SIM penetration. Comparing it to Europe misstates the baseline.

## Summary

Mobile statistics make sense only as a ladder: subscriptions (9.2 billion) exceed unique subscribers (5.83 billion) exceed smartphone users, with mobile internet users around 4.7 billion at the bottom. Penetration of 112 per 100 and coverage of 96% are both real and both routinely misread. The useful split is the coverage gap (~4%, no signal at all) versus the usage gap (~38%, signal but unaffordable), because affordability and device cost — not network rollout — now decide who is connected. Regions sit far apart on the same curve: Europe ~74% 5G coverage and North America ~79% 5G subscription penetration against Latin America ~30% coverage and a Caribbean stuck mostly on 4G. Always name the source and year — ITU, GSMA, Ericsson, and DataReportal each measure slightly differently — and remember that ITU's ~6 billion online is internet access, not GSMA's ~5.8 billion unique mobile users.

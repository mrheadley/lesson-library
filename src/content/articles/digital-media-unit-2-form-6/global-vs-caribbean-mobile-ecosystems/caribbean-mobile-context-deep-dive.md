# Caribbean Mobile Context: A Deep Dive, Country by Country

## The Two-Speed Caribbean

Every region contains internal differences; the Caribbean's are worth framing as a *two-speed* reality because they are so wide. On one side sit affluent, usually tiny, often tourism-and-finance economies that run close to developed-market standards — **Puerto Rico, the Cayman Islands, Bermuda** — with mature 5G, high postpaid rates and financed devices. On the other sits the bulk of the region — **4G, prepaid, affordability-bound**, from the OECS isles up to the laggards of **Haiti**. Between them, a few mid-size markets — **Jamaica, Trinidad, Dominican Republic** — lead the region's *English-Caribbean innovation* even while sharing the emerging-market profile.

A deep dive means visiting each of these clusters and learning what drives their success — and what blocks them.

## The "Rich Niche" Cluster: Success Through Income

The high performers are cases in the enabling power of income, not magic:

- **Puerto Rico** — the regional benchmark. Operating under US (FCC) rules with US-parity incomes, it achieved island-wide 5G (T-Mobile 2023, Claro 2024) and the Caribbean's highest **postpaid rate (~58%)**, because carrier-subsidised handset plans and device financing exist here as they do on the mainland. About **31%** of subscribers are on 5G. Puerto Rico is the answer to "what does a Caribbean market look like with developed-market money?".
- **Cayman Islands** — about **100,000 active SIMs against a 70,000 population (143% penetration)**, mature 5G on Grand Cayman (Digicel, ~22% adoption), and high postpaid (~42%). Part of the count is tourism: visitor SIMs swell the active numbers in peak season.
- **Bermuda** — about **75,000 SIMs vs. 64,000 (117%)**, postpaid ~45%, 5G targeted for 2026, powered by a financial-services employment base.

The lesson across the cluster: **income and financing, not geography or size, is what lets small islands behave like developed markets.** The same smallness that starves a poor island of infrastructure is no barrier when the residents can pay.

## The English-Caribbean Innovators: Jamaica and Trinidad

Jamaica and Trinidad are the English Caribbean's story of *first-mover* innovation within an otherwise emerging profile:

- **Jamaica** — the first English-Caribbean market to launch commercial 5G (Digicel, Kingston, October 2024), since expanded across the capital metropolitan area and the airport corridor. About **3.1 million subscriptions (112%)** with ~82% smartphone penetration. Digicel and Flow run the duopoly.
- **Trinidad & Tobago** — 5G followed in late 2024 (Digicel, Port of Spain and the East–West corridor). About **2.0 million subscriptions (~143% penetration)** with ~80% smartphone use, and a strong mobile-banking ecosystem (WiPay, blink) that makes it a fintech leader.

What makes them interesting is that they innovate *within* the constraints of the region rather than escaping them — they are the models of what a mid-size Caribbean market can build with its own rails.

## The Large-Market Counterpoint: Dominican Republic

The **Dominican Republic** is the region's largest market by subscribers — about **9.2 million active SIMs** — yet posts a lower penetration (~84%) than the small islands. The reason is not failure; it is *scale*. Ten million people dilute any per-capita count that a 70,000-strong territory inflates. Operationally it is a fuller market: three national operators (**Claro ~51%, Altice ~31%, Digicel ~18%**), 4G covering ~88% of the population, and a genuine 2025 5G spectrum auction with Claro and Altice targeting Santo Domingo by end-2026.

The Dominican Republic teaches the framework lesson in miniature: **"biggest market" and "highest penetration" are different claims** — size measures total capacity, penetration measures density, and neither ranks "better."

## The Archipelagos: Geography as a Barrier

Some Caribbean states face a difficulty most single-island markets never grapple with: the country is made of *many islands*. The **Bahamas** spans roughly 700 islands, about 30 inhabited — a network burden no single landmass carries. Its ~433,000 active SIMs (105% penetration) hide a patchwork of coverage across dozens of populated cays, and its geographic spread, not its wealth, is why 5G awaits regulator approval rather than already existing. **The Bahamas is the clearest case that "portability" and "coverage" are geographically determined** — and it is a standing warning against assuming connectivity is uniform across any Caribbean country.

## The Laggard Benchmark: Haiti

At the far end sits **Haiti** — about **60% penetration and a ~45% smartphone rate**, reliant on 3G and limited 4G with a fragile energy grid. Haiti is not a failure to be mocked; it is the *control* in the regional experiment, the benchmark for what "the gap" means at its most extreme. It demonstrates that when income is low, infrastructure and adoption both stall, and it keeps the entire discussion honest that "the Caribbean" spans the full spectrum from developed-parity to severe constraint.

## What Drives Success, and What Blocks It

Stepping back from individual countries, the deep patterns are clear.

**Success drivers:**
- **High income / tourism / finance** — Puerto Rico, Cayman, Bermuda (financing, postpaid, 5G).
- **A diaspora** — remittances, an outsized audience, investor interest and returning talent (Jamaica, and effectively the whole English Caribbean).
- **A coherent small market with real competition** — a focused duopoly or trio can still innovate (Jamaica, Trinidad, DR).

**Barriers:**
- **Small populations** — weak economies of scale; infrastructure costs more per user.
- **Archipelagos** — Bahamas-style geography raises per-user cost and reduces redundancy.
- **Low income** — Haiti: no financing, no devices, no demand pull.
- **Thin local production & capital** — a heavy adoption base with little local value capture (the production gap from Lesson 104).
- **Policy/regulatory lag** — 5G deferred where spectrum is slow (Bahamas, Barbados) regardless of operator readiness.

## Common Misconceptions

### "All Caribbean countries are at a similar stage"
The spread is enormous — Haiti at ~60% to Cayman above 140%, no-5G to island-wide-5G. Country-by-country is the only honest view.

### "Small islands always lag"
Some small high-income islands (Cayman, Bermuda, Puerto Rico) lead on 5G and postpaid. Smallness hurts, but income helps more.

### "Bigger islands have the best connectivity"
The DR, though largest, has mid-tier penetration. Scale brings more subscribers, not necessarily better per-user outcomes.

### "5G available means the whole market uses 5G"
Even where it launched, adoption is early (Cayman ~22%, Puerto Rico ~31%) — most traffic remains 4G.

### "Success here = copying another country's playbook"
Each market's profile (population, income, diaspora, geography, policy) dictates what works. Copy-paste fails.

## Summary

The Caribbean is a two-speed region: affluent niches (Puerto Rico, Cayman, Bermuda) run on income, financing and 5G; the bulk runs on 4G and prepaid; mid-size innovators (Jamaica, Trinidad) lead within the constraints; the Dominican Republic shows scale versus density; archipelagos like the Bahamas show geography as a barrier; Haiti is the laggard benchmark. Success clusters around income, tourism/finance, and diaspora; barriers around small size, archipelagos, low income, thin production and regulatory lag. The task of a deep dive is to read any *one* country as a combination of assets and barriers across the four layers — and to understand that no single island's playbook transfers unchanged to another.

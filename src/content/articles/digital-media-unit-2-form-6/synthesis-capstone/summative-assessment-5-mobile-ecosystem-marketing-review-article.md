# Summative Assessment 5: Mobile Ecosystem & Marketing — Review Article

## What This Summative Covers

Summative 5 brings together **Sections 6 and 7** — the mobile ecosystem and the marketing/distribution/monetisation journey. It is sat in a single period (up to 30 minutes of assessment), so your preparation happens here, independently. This review is your study guide: it does **not** re-teach the lessons; it connects them, flags where students most often stumble, and shows you the one journey that runs through both sections.

The single idea that ties it all together:

> **An app's success is a chain — a real local need → a UX that fits the region's devices and bandwidth → built with the right tools → distributed and marketed for the region's real platform mix → monetised with a model that fits its users.** (Lessons 144, 169)

Hold onto that sentence; everything below hangs off it.

---

## Key Concepts Review

### The Mobile Ecosystem (Lessons 20, 32–40, 91–99)

A mobile **ecosystem** is the interrelated set of players that make mobile use possible and valuable: **content + infrastructure + devices + users + carriers + policy.** No player succeeds alone.

The **Android vs iOS** distinction remains central and is tested as *reasoning*, not preference:

- **Android** — open platform: Google Play, device fragmentation, wider distribution (APK/sideloading), more flexibility and responsibility.
- **iOS** — closed platform: App Store, strict review, tight integration, more control and consistency.

**Know which behaviours follow from "open" vs "closed."**

### Global vs Caribbean Infrastructure (Lessons 100–108)

The Caribbean has **high smartphone adoption and high social penetration (WhatsApp ~85%+)** but **constrained data plans, bandwidth, and device floors.** The recurring test point: **design must be built down to the region's real reality, not up to a global ideal.** Global players use high 4G/5G and mature supply chains; the region needs constraint-led design.

### Local Needs, Mobile UX & App Build (Lessons 109–135)

Local apps serve culture, news, services, and community; regional needs (disaster prep, health, education, agri) matter. The UX constraints to know cold: **screen size, bandwidth, portability, processing power, accessibility.** The build path: **conceptualisation/user stories → wireframes → SDKs → native vs cross-platform (React Native/Flutter) → no-code/low-code → testing → store submission.** Choose by need, not hype.

### Marketing Strategies & Distribution (Lessons 147–161)

- **M1 promotional strategies** — guerrilla, viral, cross-platform, email, influencer.
- **Distribution** — choose the store/platform and do the ASO; distribution sets the marketing's job (**integration**).
- **Marketing** — the funnel (awareness → activation → retention), analytics, and the economics test **LTV > CAC** (users must generate more value than they cost to acquire).
- **Channels are a quality decision** — the acquisition source affects churn.

### Revenue Models (Lessons 162–168)

Choose by **fit** (usage pattern, audience, value type), not fashion. The family:

- **Paid, freemium, IAP** (consumable vs non-consumable), **ads**, **subscriptions**, **mission-funded** (open source/donation/civic).
- Remember the economics: freemium converts ~2–5%; ads are a volume/low-per-user stream; subscriptions give 4–6× the LTV but fight churn; annual plans cut churn ~40%; hybrids lift LTV.
- **The LTV > CAC rule and retention** are the through-line for judging any plan.

## The Likely Trouble Spots (where students most often trip)

1. **Open vs closed / Android vs iOS.** Don't treat it as "which is nicer." Connect each behaviour (review, sideloading, fragmentation) to the *open/closed philosophy*.
2. **Getting the local-vs-global story backwards.** A global audience is not automatically better; mobile-first/local-first is constraint-led design.
3. **Choosing a model by fashion, not fit.** "Subscriptions are best" is false — justify by usage/audience/value with a real figure.
4. **Forgetting LTV vs CAC.** The sustainability test is central; a plan that ignores whether users out-earn their acquisition cost is structurally weak.
5. **Confusing the models.** Know the difference between consumable/non-consumable IAP, the ad formats (rewarded ≫ banner), the subscription/churn physics, and mission-funded funding.
6. **Forgetting the regional lens.** Caribbean reality (data budget, mobile money ~a third, WhatsApp-led) refits distribution, marketing, and monetisation.
7. **Recalling sections in isolation.** Both sections are one journey — be able to walk the chain from a local need to a monetisation model.

## How to Prepare (self-study)

Revise by **connecting**, not just rereading. For each of your SBA/app products (or sample cases):

- Walk the full chain: need → UX → build → distribute → market → monetise.
- For a given app, **choose a revenue model and justify it** (usage, audience, value, a benchmark, why not the alternatives).
- Run the economics: is LTV > CAC? Would it survive a Caribbean data/payment reality?

Use the three suggested self-study resources alongside this review to test yourself, then check the trouble spots above. The assessment rewards the person who can reason the whole journey with a named fact at each stage — not the person who memorised the most names.

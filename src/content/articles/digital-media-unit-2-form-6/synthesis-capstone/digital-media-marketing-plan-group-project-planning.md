# The Digital Media Marketing Plan: Planning a Complete, Defensible Document

## What the Capstone Is

This is the **Section 7 capstone**: a group project in which your team writes a comprehensive **digital media marketing plan** for a given case scenario — integrating everything from distribution, marketing, and monetisation. This session is the **planning** of that plan: structuring a complete, evidence-backed document (polish and presentation come in the next lesson).

The plan is the living application of the whole Section 7 journey:

> Identify the target audience → choose distribution/channels → set the funnel & measurement → decide the revenue model → confirm the economics (LTV vs CAC, retention) → plan the launch

## The Anatomy of a Complete Marketing Plan

A real plan is more than a list of social channels. It covers, in order:

1. **Target audience & value proposition** — *who* the case's users are and *what value* they get. This is the foundation: every later decision derives from it.
2. **Distribution choices** — the store/platform, ASO work (Lessons 154–156).
3. **Marketing channels & funnel strategy** — paid, influencer, social, content (Lessons 157–158).
4. **Analytics & measurement plan** — the specific metrics you'll track, and how (Lessons 159–160).
5. **Revenue model with justification and economics** — the model, the fit, and the LTV vs CAC expectation (Lessons 162–168).
6. **Launch timeline + budget** — sequenced (pre-launch → launch → iterate), scoped to reality.

## Audience First

Start from **who** the case's users are and **what value** they get, *then* derive channels and model. A plan that names its audience and the need it serves has a defensible core; a plan that picks channels by habit has none. This echoes the needs-first discipline from the course's early lessons — the plan is only as strong as its audience understanding.

## Build It With Evidence, Not Guesses

Every section is a **justified choice**, not an opinion. Where you decide a channel or a model, cite the reason and, where useful, a real figure from the course:

- Named the audience → explain *why this channel reaches them*.
- Chose a revenue model → run the **fit test** (usage, audience, value) and **justify** it (recycling your choose-and-justify reasoning from Lesson 167).
- Stated economics → write one line on **LTV vs CAC** and how you'll measure it. A plan whose acquisition cost or monetisation is unexamined is structurally weak.

## Plan the Measurement

"See how it does" is not a measurement plan. Decide the **funnel stages** and the **specific metrics** to track — CPI/CAC, activation, retention, eCPM/ARPU, churn — so the plan can actually be evaluated later. This is the accountability-as-representation of the capstone: a plan you can check against numbers, not hope against vibes.

## Scope It to Reality (Budget & Timeline)

A believable plan is **scoped** — it spends within what the case allows and sequences the work. An unfundable fantasy or a globally-blind plan is weak. Show a realistic budget and a sequenced launch, and let the regional reality shape the scale.

## Regional Fit Is Part of the Plan

For a local case, account for **Caribbean reality** in both channel and model:

- WhatsApp-led, data-light distribution.
- Relevance and fit over a global volume play.
- Freemium/low-cost/ads or mission-funded over a high global subscription, given purchasing power and ~a third mobile-money penetration.

A plan that survives the regional lens is the believable one.

## Working This Lesson's Group Activity

With a group of your size, structure roles so everyone contributes and the volume is manageable:

- **Strategy lead** — the audience, value, and overall direction.
- **Research/evidence lead** — the benchmarks and reasons behind each decision.
- **Economics lead** — the LTV vs CAC and measurement numbers.
- **Design/presentation lead** — how the plan is laid out and will be presented.

Assign responsibilities explicitly, build each section together, and the next lesson's presentation and evaluation will run smoothly.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"A marketing plan is just a list of social channels."** It spans audience, distribution, channels, measurement, revenue model, and economics — the Section 7 chain, not a channel list.
- **"Writing the plan down is enough."** Each decision must be justified with evidence (fit, benchmark, LTV>CAC) or it fails the accountability bar.
- **"Revenue model is a separate appendix."** Monetisation is the economic heart — tie it to audience, channel, and LTV.
- **"Bigger budget / global channels = better plan."** A plan must be scoped and regionally realistic.
- **"The quietest member can sit out."** Assigned, explicit roles guarantee each member contributes.

## Summary

Plan the Section 7 capstone as a complete, evidence-backed marketing plan: start from the audience and value, cover distribution, channels, measurement, revenue model with economics, and a scoped launch timeline + budget. Justify every decision (including a one-line LTV vs CAC and measurement plan), apply the regional lens throughout, and structure group work with explicit roles. A plan you can defend with numbers is the deliverable — built here, presented and evaluated next lesson.

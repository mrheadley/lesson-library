# Section 7 Review: The Whole Journey of Taking an App to Market

## What This Review Covers

This is a **review** lesson — not new content. It pulls Section 7 together before your summative, using revenue-model case studies as the active way to consolidate. Section 7 is one journey with three linked stages:

> **Distribution** (get it in reach) → **Marketing** (convert & keep) → **Revenue** (make it pay)

And the single currency that links all three is **retention and lifetime value (LTV)**. Hold onto that whole-sentence frame; everything below hangs off it.

## The Three Stages, Connected

**1. Distribution (Lessons 154–156).** Choose the right store/platform and optimise the product page (ASO). Distribution gets the app *in reach* — but it is only the start of the funnel. The channel you choose sets the job your marketing must do (this is the integration point of Lesson 161).

**2. Marketing (Lessons 157–160).** Paid, influencer, social, and content strategies convert attention. The funnel (awareness → activation → retention) and analytics decide whether the spend works. The economics test is **LTV > CAC** — users must generate more value than they cost to acquire (Lesson 160).

**3. Revenue (Lessons 162–168).** Choose a model by fit (usage pattern, audience, value type), not fashion: paid, freemium, IAP, ads, subscriptions, or mission-funded. Each has named economics — freemium converts ~2–5%, ads are a volume/low-per-user stream, subscriptions give 4–6× the LTV but fight churn, annual plans cut churn ~40%, hybrids lift LTV.

## Why the Sections Won't Work Apart

A revenue model only "works" if the acquisition funnel holds and users stay long enough to pay or engage. That is why **retention and LTV** are the through-line:

- A loud marketing campaign that brings users who never stick is a loss (Lesson 160) — the acquisition channel is a *quality* decision.
- A great revenue model is worthless if nobody uses the app (retention) or if acquiring users costs more than they're worth (CAC > LTV).
- This is why distribution + marketing **feed** the revenue thread: they hand you users, and monetisation decides those users' worth.

**Worked example — the one-chain check:** a game app that ships to the right store (distribution), runs a tight influencer campaign to a gaming audience (marketing), and monetises the high-intent players with rewarded video + consumable IAP (revenue). Each stage amplifies the next; break any stage and the chain breaks.

## The Case-Study Consolidation

The active vehicle for this review is the **case-study method**: re-run a few of the revenue-model winner/loser pairs from Lesson 168 as the practice. For each, run the checks:

1. **Fit** — did the model match usage/audience/value?
2. **Economics** — LTV vs CAC?
3. **Retention** — did users stay?

This re-anchors the LTV>CAC rule and the model-taxonomy lessons in concrete examples rather than abstracts.

## Regional Reality Throughout

The Caribbean lens refits every Section 7 decision:

- **Distribution** is WhatsApp-led and data-light.
- **Marketing** leads with relevance and fit over a global volume play.
- **Revenue** refits to purchasing power (freemium/low-cost/ads often fit better than a high global subscription), payment infrastructure (~a third mobile-money penetration), and low data budgets.

A launch should be **regional first**, in all three stages — not a copy of a big-market template.

## The Trust / Ethics Through-Line

Dark-pattern monetisation (forced ads, hidden subscriptions) damages retention and reputation — revenue *must* respect the user. This is the accountability/trust thread woven through the whole course: trust is part of the economics, not separate from it.

## Likely Trouble Spots

- **Treating Section 7 as a list of tactics.** It is one journey; the review makes the chain visible.
- **Thinking revenue is the only point.** Distribution and marketing decide whether anyone reaches the revenue stage.
- **Believing a single model or tactic is best.** Fit and economics decide; there's no one-size answer.
- **Copying global cases wholesale.** Caribbean economics and platform mix refit everything.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Section 7 is a stack of separate tactics."** It is one linked journey (distribution → marketing → revenue) unified by retention/LTV.
- **"Revenue is the only point of going to market."** Without distribution and marketing nobody reaches the revenue stage.
- **"Subscriptions are automatically best."** Fit and economics decide; there is no universal winner.
- **"Reviewing is just re-reading."** Synthesis means connecting ideas across lessons — the activities force those connections.
- **"Global cases transfer directly."** Regional reality refits every decision.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Section 7 journey | Distribution → marketing → monetisation, unified by retention/LTV |
| Model fit | Choosing a model by usage, audience, value — not fashion |
| LTV > CAC | The sustainability condition; a model loses per user when CAC exceeds LTV |
| Sustainability | LTV > CAC (commercial) or a viable funding mix (civic/OSS) |
| Case-study lesson | A transferable insight from a real economic outcome |

## Summary

Section 7 is one journey — **distribution → marketing → revenue** — linked by retention and LTV. Distribution gets the app in reach, marketing converts and keeps users (so watch LTV > CAC and retention, not just installs), and revenue monetises those users by a model chosen for fit. Use the case-study checklist (fit, economics, retention) to consolidate, apply the regional lens throughout, and remember trust is part of the economics. Prepare for the summative by being able to walk the whole journey with a named fact at each stage.

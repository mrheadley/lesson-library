# Revenue Model Case Studies: Learning From What Paid Off — and What Didn't

## Why Study Cases

The earlier revenue lessons taught you the **models** and their **economics**. This lesson applies the case-study method (the discipline you met in the trends and marketing-case lessons) to revenue: studying real apps that chose a model, ran its economics, and succeeded or struggled — to extract transferable lessons for your own app.

The lens is the same as the marketing-case lesson: not just "did it make money?" but **"did the model fit the app, and did its economics hold?"** Success and failure are equally instructive — a case that churned into oblivion teaches as much as one that scaled.

## The Economic Through-Line: Check Fit, LTV, and Retention

In every case, run the checks you already own:

- **Fit** — did the model match the app's usage pattern, audience, and value type (from the model lessons)?
- **LTV vs CAC** — is the user generating more value than they cost to acquire (from the marketing-case lesson)?
- **Retention** — did users stay long enough to pay or engage? A model can "work" yet still fail if retention collapses.

A headline "it made money!" is not an ending — check the whole economics before believing it.

## Patterns That Recur Across the Model Families

**Subscription winners vs losers:**
- **Winners** earn renewal with continuous value + transparent billing — annual plans cut churn ~40%, and strong Month-2 retention sustains very long average lives.
- **Losers** hit **subscription fatigue** (users hold ~7 subscriptions) by adding little real recurring value, or use confusing/opaque billing that breeds angry churn.

**Freemium / IAP:**
- Winners monetise high-intent users and "whales" with consumables/non-consumables priced at recognised value; they give away enough to funnel but not so much they cannibalise.
- Losers either give away everything (no one needs to pay) or choke the funnel (no one stays long enough to feel value).

**Advertising:**
- Winners use **rewarded video** (opt-in, high completion, top eCPM) balanced against UX, or run a hybrid ad-free option.
- Losers spam interstitials — short-term revenue that chases away users and drags ASO.

**Mission/civic (open source / public good):**
- Winners pair an open core with a deliberate **sustainability mix** — sponsorships + donations + grants (e.g. NLNet, Sovereign Tech Fund, ecosystem grant programs).
- Losers stall from volunteer burnout when funding is ignored — for them, *sustainability funding* is the revenue model.

## Worked Example: Reading a Pair

**Case A — the subscription that stayed.** An app with one clear recurring value (e.g. a study planner) delivers Day-1 value, uses an annual plan to cut churn, and has strong Month-2 retention. Users stay, LTV exceeds CAC, and the model fits the daily-use pattern. **Lesson:** a fitting model + value-delivery + churn management = sustainable recurring revenue.

**Case B — the subscription that churned.** The same daily-use app, but it added a subscription on top of low recurring value, with a hidden auto-renew and no easy cancel. Users feel misled, churn is high, many are angry "involuntary-plus" leavers, and the store's rating drops — hurting ASO and future acquisition. **Lesson:** monetisation without trust destroys retention; value-delivery and transparency are part of the economics.

Lessons worth keeping, not verdicts: "annual + transparent beats monthly + opaque"; "fit precedes revenue"; "revenue without retention is just churn in fancy clothes."

## The Trust / Ethics Layer

Several failure cases are really **dark-pattern** cases — forced ads, hidden subscriptions, hard-to-find cancellation. These teach the connection to your accountability and trust work: **revenue that disrespects the user destroys the relationship that made the revenue possible.** Monetisation is part of the ethical product, not something separate from it.

## Regional Reality: Cases Don't Transfer Neatly

Every case is global; the **transferable lesson** is the mechanism (fit, churn, fatigue, sustainability, LTV>CAC), not the specific numbers. Refit before copying:

- A US-priced subscription business may not transfer to a market with lower purchasing power and ~a third mobile-money penetration.
- A data-heavy ad campaign may not fit low-data users.
- Regional models are refitted: a freemium/ad path + localised pricing often beats a copy of a premium global subscription.

## Extract Lessons for Your Own App

Close by making it yours: write **three "revenue lessons for our app"** statements distilled from the cases, feeding your choose-and-justify (Lesson 167) and your marketing plan (Lessons 170–171). Studying someone else's model is only useful once it changes your own plan.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"A model that made money in a case is a safe model."** Economic fit, churn, and regional reality decide; a profitable US subscription can fail locally.
- **"The app with the biggest revenue is the best case."** Revenue without sustainable LTV>CAC and retention is a failure-in-progress.
- **"Ads made the case fail / subscriptions made it succeed."** Execution and fit (dark patterns, value-delivery, churn) decide — not the model label.
- **"Company cases don't apply to a student app."** The transferable mechanisms apply at any scale — that's the point of a case.
- **"Names of companies are the lesson."** The lesson is the *mechanism*; company names are just evidence, not takeaways.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Revenue-model case study | Analysis of a real app's model + economic outcome, producing transferable lessons |
| Monetisation fit | Whether the model matched usage/audience/value |
| Whale / high-intent user | The few users (esp. games) who spend far more |
| Sustainability mix | The funding sources (sponsorship/donation/grants) keeping a non-commercial app alive |
| Transferable lesson | The general, app-agnostic insight, not a factual replay |

## Summary

Study real revenue cases with the case-study discipline and the economic through-line (fit, LTV>CAC, retention). Learn from both winners and losers across each model family — subscription, freemium/IAP, ads, mission-funded — and from dark-pattern failures that show why trust is part of the economics. Refit every case to regional reality rather than copying the numbers, and finish by writing three "revenue lessons for our own app" that feed your justification and marketing plan. Evidence plus economics equals a defensible revenue decision.

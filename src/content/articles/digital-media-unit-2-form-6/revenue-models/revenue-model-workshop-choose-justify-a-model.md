# Choose a Revenue Model and Justify It: A Decision Workshop Guide

## What This Lesson Is For

You now know the full family of revenue models (paid, freemium, IAP, advertising, subscriptions, mission-funded) and the economics behind them. The workshop lesson flips from *learning* to *deciding*: you pick a revenue model for your own app and **justify it with evidence**.

This is not a new-content lesson — it is the place where the models become a **decision you can defend**. And because it is assessed, the skill on the table is precisely your ability to reason about the choice, not just to name a popular model.

## The Choice Discipline: Fit, Not Fashion

The temptation is to reach for whatever sounds impressive ("our app uses subscriptions!"). Resist it. The decision rule from the overview lesson:

> Pick the model that fits **your app's** usage pattern, audience, and value type — not what's trendy.

Run your app through the **fit test**:

1. **Usage frequency & session length** — does the user use it daily and long, or rarely and briefly?
   - Daily, long, ongoing → **subscription**.
   - Short, occasional → **ads or IAP/paid**.
2. **Audience size & type** — mass and price-sensitive, or niche and pay-willing?
   - Mass, sensitive → **ads/freemium**.
   - Niche, professional, willing → **paid/subscription**.
3. **Value type** — one-off, ongoing, or episodic-burst?
   - Ongoing service → **subscription**.
   - One-off → **paid/IAP**.
   - Episodic burst (games) → **consumable IAP**.

## Make the Case With Evidence and Benchmarks

A justified choice is not an opinion — it cites real figures to support the reasoning. Use the benchmarks from the revenue lessons as your evidence:

- Choose **freemium**? Quote the free-to-paid conversion (2–5%) and explain how the free tier funnels the *right* users.
- Choose **ads**? Reference that ads are a volume, low-per-user stream, and name the format (rewarded ≫ banner) you'd use.
- Choose **subscriptions**? Mention ~4–6× the LTV per user, the churn fight, and annual plans cutting churn ~40%.
- Choose **mission-funded**? Explain the sustainability funding (sponsorship/donations/grants) rather than customer transactions.

**The assessment bar:** a strong justification names a primary model (+ hybrid where appropriate), links the choice to the app's usage/audience/value, quotes a relevant figure, names one weakness and one alternative, and accounts for the regional reality. Address *why this and not one of the other four.*

## Should You Run a Hybrid?

Hybrid is common but not automatic. **Stack** streams when your users genuinely differ in willingness to pay (some tolerate ads, some pay for ad-free). Adding complexity is only worth it if it captures value a single model leaves on the table. Justify hybrid explicitly — "we run free-with-ads plus a pay-to-remove option, because our audience splits into users who won't pay and users who will" — not "hybrid sounds advanced."

## The Regional Reality Check

Model choice is regional. The question that hardens a justification:

> Would this still work for a Caribbean user on a low data budget, using mobile money, in a price-sensitive market?

If the answer is "no," the fit test isn't finished — refit the model (a freemium/low-cost or ad path may beat a high-priced global subscription locally). A justification that survives the regional lens is a real one.

## The Mission-Funded Option Is a Valid Choice

Remember: if your app is genuinely a public good, **mission-funded is not "no model"** — it is a deliberate, defensible choice with its own logic and sustainability funding. Do not feel pushed into monetising users if the app's purpose is civic/public service. Justify it as seriously as you would a subscription.

## Deliverable: Your Choose-and-Justify

Your written output should be an argument, roughly structured:

1. **The app** — one line: who it serves, the core value.
2. **The fit test** — its usage pattern, audience, value type.
3. **The chosen model + evidence** — the model (and hybrid if any) and the figure that supports it.
4. **Why not the alternatives** — one honest reason each for the two most plausible rivals.
5. **One weakness + the alternative** — what could go wrong, and the model you'd fall back to.
6. **The regional check** — why this still fits the local reality.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Choosing the most popular model (subscription) proves it's right."** Subscription is highest-LTV but not right for every app — the justification must link the choice to *this* app.
- **"A model is justified by naming it."** The skill is the reason — who pays, how, what value, what cost.
- **"Hybrid is always better / always worse."** Hybrid lifts LTV when segments differ in willingness-to-pay but adds complexity; it's a considered choice, not a default.
- **"Only profit models are real options."** Mission-funded is legitimate and defensible for public-good apps — fit, not profit, drives the right answer.
- **"One global model works everywhere."** Buying power, payment infrastructure, and data budgets change what fits.
- **"The workshop ends when you pick."** The justification is the assigned, marked output — the reasoning is the product.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Model fit | Matching the model to usage pattern, audience, and value type |
| Hybrid model | Deliberately stacking streams to capture different willingness-to-pay |
| Justification | The evidence-linked defence: who pays, how often, what value, what cost |
| Decision rubric | The structured criteria (usage, audience, value + economics) that test the choice |
| Regional fit | Checking the model survives local payment/purchasing-power/data reality |

## Summary

Choosing a revenue model is a decision you must defend, not a name you assert. Use the fit test — usage frequency, audience type, value type — to pick, then **support it with a real benchmark** and a reason *why not* the alternatives. Consider hybrid only where user segments genuinely differ, apply the regional reality check, and remember mission-funded is a valid choice for public-good apps. The assigned justification is the point: evidence plus reasoning equals a defensible revenue decision.

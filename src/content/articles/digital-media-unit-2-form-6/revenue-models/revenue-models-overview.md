# Revenue Models: Who Pays, How, and for What?

## The Question This Lesson Answers

You have learned how to **acquire** users (marketing) and **deliver** an app (distribution). Now the crucial follow-up: **what are those users worth, and how does the app actually make money?** This is the *revenue* thread, and Lesson 162 starts it with the big picture.

The one question that frames the whole thread:

> *Who pays, how, and for what value?*

A **revenue model** is exactly that — the mechanism by which an app turns value into money. This lesson gives you the **taxonomy** (the family of models) and the **decision rule** for choosing among them. It takes the overview you met in Lesson 40 and builds it out to full depth.

## The Set of Models (the Taxonomy)

There is no single "best" model — there is a family, and you pick by fit. The main families:

- **Paid upfront** — the user pays once to download and keep the app.
- **Freemium** — a genuinely useful free version plus paid upgrades.
- **In-app purchases (IAP)** — buying consumable (used up, e.g. coins/lives) or non-consumable (permanent, e.g. ad removal) items inside the app.
- **In-app advertising** — displaying paid ads inside the app to monetise inventory.
- **Subscriptions** — a recurring fee for ongoing access/value.
- **Mission-funded** — open source, donations, civic/public-good funding (purpose-driven rather than profit-driven).

Mature apps rarely pick one cold; they run a **hybrid** — stacking two or more streams (for example a free ad-supported tier, an ad-free subscription, and optional IAP) to capture different users' willingness to pay.

## The Market Reality (2026 — taught as posture, not exact dollars)

These figures move and vary by month and platform, so treat them as **directional**: they tell you *relative scale*, which is what matters for choosing a model.

- The app economy is enormous and still dominated by **advertising as the largest single stream** (~65% of app revenue) — but ads are a **volume** model: big absolute totals, low value **per user**.
- **Freemium** is the dominant strategy among top-grossing apps, with a typically low free-to-paid **conversion rate of 2–5%**.
- **Subscriptions** are the fastest-growing and highest-value-per-user family (**4–6× the lifetime value of ads**), yet used by only a small fraction of apps and now slowed a little by subscription fatigue.
- **Paid-upfront is marginal**: under ~3% of downloads are paid — the money is overwhelmingly in free apps monetised another way.
- **IAP** is led by gaming, where only a small share of users convert — monetisation optimises the high-intent users who do spend.

The striking headline to remember: **the stores take 15–30% of digital revenue** (30% standard, reduced to 15% later for some subscriptions). So the "price" you set is not what you keep — you must price *through* the store fee.

## The Decision Rule: Model Fits the App, Not Fashion

Here is the choice discipline, and it is the heart of the lesson:

- **Session length & frequency** — how long and how often do users engage? Short or occasional sessions suit **ads or paid/IAP**; long, daily, ongoing use suits **subscriptions**.
- **Audience size & type** — a mass, price-sensitive audience suits **ads/freemium**; a niche, professional, pay-willing audience suits **paid/subscription**.
- **Value type** — is the value one-off (fits paid/IAP), ongoing/recurring (fits subscription), or episodic-burst (fits consumable IAP)?

And a final layer: **is the audience willing and able to pay?** — which is where regional reality (purchasing power, payment infrastructure, data budgets) enters the decision.

**Worked example — the metric-to-model match:**
| App description | Sessions | Audience | Natural model |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-time calculator | rare | broad, low intent | paid / one-time |
| Daily habit tracker | daily, long | committed | subscription |
| Casual mass game | short bursts, many | mass | ads + consumable IAP (hybrid) |
| Niche pro tool | occasional | small, pay-willing | subscription / paid |

The pattern is: **start from your app's usage, audience, and value — not from what's trendy.** A model that looks exciting but doesn't fit your users will not perform.

## Regional Reality: Refit, Don't Copy

In the Caribbean, purchasing power, payment infrastructure (only about a third of people with access to mobile money in the region), constrained data budgets, and WhatsApp-led usage change which model fits. A high-priced global subscription model may not fit a local audience; a **freemium/low-cost or ad-supported path** often fits better. Model choice is a regional decision, not a global one.

## Why "Monetise Last" Is a Trap

The strongest lesson from the industry is not to treat revenue as an afterthought. **Apps that design monetisation last are among those that fail** — you should decide the model with the product, because the value you build dictates (and limits) the money you can make. But equally, revenue without **product-market fit** fails: even great monetisation cannot rescue an app nobody actually needs. Fit precedes monetisation.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Paid apps make the most money."** False: under ~3% of downloads are paid, and the money is in freemium/IAP/ads/subscriptions on free apps.
- **"Subscriptions are best for everything."** False: subscription fatigue and category fit mean many apps (games, one-time tools) are better served by IAP, ads, or paid. Pick by usage pattern, not fashion.
- **"One model is clearly best."** False: mature apps stack (hybrid); different users have different willingness to pay.
- **"The app keeps 100% of its price."** False: stores take 15–30% of digital revenue; price through the fee.
- **"Advertising is the easiest, therefore best."** Ads have the biggest absolute revenue but low per-user value; it only works at scale with big audiences — model fit matters.
- **"A revenue model is an afterthought."** Revenue designed last is a leading cause of failed apps; decide the model with the product.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Revenue model | The mechanism converting value into money: who pays, how, for what |
| Taxonomy / model family | The set of models: paid, freemium, IAP, ads, subscriptions, mission-funded |
| Hybrid model | Stacking 2+ streams to capture different willingness-to-pay |
| ARPU / LTV / CPM-eCPM | Unit metrics for revenue decisions (per-user revenue; lifetime value; cost per thousand impressions) |
| Model fit | Matching the model to usage pattern, audience, and value type, not fashion |
| Store fee | The 15–30% cut the stores take of digital revenue |

## Summary

A revenue model answers *who pays, how, and for what value.* The options form a taxonomy — paid, freemium, IAP, advertising, subscriptions, and mission-funded — and mature apps combine them into hybrids. There is no universal "best"; the decision rule is fit: match the model to the app's session length/frequency, audience size/type, and value type, and to whether the audience is willing and able to pay. Remember the economics (ads are huge in total but low per user; subscriptions are high per user; stores take 15–30%) and the regional reality (a freemium/low-cost or ad path may fit the Caribbean better than a global subscription). Decide the model with the product, not after it — but only after you have real product-market fit.

# Devices, Users & Carriers

## Where the Ecosystem Meets a Real Person

Every layer you've met so far — content, infrastructure, networks — finally converges on a single point: the **end user**, holding a device, buying a plan from a carrier, and using the network. That retail meeting point is the subject of this lesson. It comes down to three things: **what people hold** (devices), **who the users are** (segments), and **who provides the line into the network** (carriers).

Follow a single connection as it reaches a person:

1. A **physical device** — a smartphone, tablet, or feature phone.
2. A **SIM** (Subscriber Identity Module) that ties that device and user to a carrier.
3. A **radio link** from the device into a licensed operator's network.
4. A **billing relationship** — your prepaid credit or monthly plan with the carrier.
5. Out to the **wider internet**, where the content and infrastructure layers live.

Every step in that chain is a business decision, not just a technical one.

## Device Categories

Devices are not one thing. They form a spectrum from high-end smartphones down to basic feature phones.

| Device | What it is | Who uses it |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Smartphone | Full touchscreen, app stores, fast mobile broadband | The majority of users — ~89% of handsets in use are smartphones |
| Tablet | Larger touchscreen for consuming and creating | Users who want a bigger screen on Wi-Fi or mobile data |
| Feature phone | Basic calls/text, limited or no internet apps | Lower-income segments; smartphones out of budget for many |

The global picture: smartphone shipments grew about **2% in 2025 to roughly 1.25–1.26 billion units**, the highest since 2021. Trackers differ slightly on exact figures, but all agree on the ranking.

| Brand | Approx. 2025 share | Approx. units |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| Apple | ~20% | ~248 million |
| Samsung | ~19% | ~241 million |
| Xiaomi | ~13% | ~165 million |
| vivo / OPPO | top-five | — |

The precise shares vary a little between IDC, Omdia, and Counterpoint, and flagship brand statistics differ across trackers — but **Apple edged out Samsung for #1 in 2025 and Xiaomi was #3** in all of them. On the software side, **Android runs ~72% of devices globally vs. iOS ~27%** (DataReportal/Fosspost).

There are roughly **7.3–7.6 billion smartphone subscriptions** in 2025, forecast to exceed **~7.9 billion by 2028**, led by China, India, and the US.

## Caribbean Devices in Practice

A Caribbean student might carry a budget Android (say, a Redmi) and covet an iPhone — that tension is exactly **device segmentation** in action. Brand reach varies by region and price point: Apple and Samsung lead globally, but Android-friendly Chinese brands and region-specific players (like Claro in the Dominican Republic) dominate particular segments. In the English-speaking Caribbean, Digicel's ecosystem and Flow's offerings shape which devices people actually buy.

A family sharing one mobile hotspot to get homework done online is a perfect illustration of the **"mobile-first home broadband"** model common in the region: for many Caribbean households, the mobile connection *is* the home internet. Feature phones still matter across lower-income segments, where a smartphone remains out of budget.

## User Segments

Users aren't one blob either. They're divided by age, income, data habits, and price sensitivity. Two Caribbean traits stand out:

- **Data-first behaviour** — users reach for content and apps as much as calls and texts.
- **Heavy prepaid usage** — paying as-you-go rather than on a monthly bill.

These traits shape everything carriers build: bundle structures, "top-up" instincts, and dual-SIM workarounds.

## Carriers: Prepaid vs. Postpaid

Carriers (Mobile Network Operators, or MNOs) convert spectrum and network investment into retail service. Their two core business models:

- **Prepaid**: you buy credit in advance — the "top-up" model. Dominates the Caribbean.
- **Postpaid**: you're billed monthly for a plan.

Prepaid generally accounts for **60–80% of subscribers** in most Caribbean markets (Hope Research Group). That's a striking and deliberate regional pattern — and it explains why Caribbean phone culture revolves around bundles and recharges rather than contracts and bills.

Many operators also pursue **convergence** — bundling broadband, mobile, and pay-TV together. Liberty Latin America (Flow) uses this strategy across the Caribbean, offering households one relationship for all their connectivity.

## The Caribbean Carrier Landscape

Caribbean markets are dominated by a few operators with high penetration.

| Market | Active SIMs vs population | Penetration | Leading operator(s) | 5G status |
|--------|---------------------------|-------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Trinidad & Tobago | ~2.02M vs ~1.41M | ~143% | Digicel ~52%; Digicel, Flow, bmobile | Launched in Port of Spain late 2024 |
| Jamaica | ~3.16M (Q3 2025) | — | Digicel ~47%, rest Flow | Kingston Q4 2024, expanding 2025 |
| Barbados | ~333k vs ~282k | ~118% | Digicel ~55% | Spectrum approved late 2025, launch ~H2 2026 |

These penetration figures are rough — the Jamaica active-SIM count differs by year and method across sources — but the pattern holds: penetration **over 100%** in most Caribbean markets. That number is not "everyone has a phone." It reflects **dual-SIM owners** carrying more than one line.

A student carrying a Digicel SIM for some things and a Flow or bmobile SIM for others is a familiar Caribbean workaround for managing cost and coverage. Dual-SIM use is so common it **skews penetration figures above 100%** — which is why 108%-plus penetration averages don't mean universal device ownership.

## MVNOs: Carriers Without Towers

Not every provider needs its own spectrum and towers. An **MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator)** is a service provider that leases capacity from an established MNO and resells it, without owning the physical network.

This matters because MVNOs are a way to **increase competition and reach niche segments** without the huge cost of building infrastructure. They've been emerging across the Caribbean:

- **Trinidad and Tobago** ran its first MVNO licensing round in 2024 under TATT.
- **Barbados** has one active MVNO.
- **Jamaica** has discussed but not yet launched commercial MVNOs.
- **Wizzee**, a digital-first eSIM operator launched by Digicel for the French West Indies (Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Saint-Martin, Saint-Barthélemy), is a regional MVNO-style model — it moved fully to eSIM in July 2025.

MVNOs are the "virtual" side of mobile: they prove you can be a real player in the ecosystem without owning a single tower.

## New Access, New Reach

Carriers and new access models are changing how users get online. After Hurricane Melissa in 2025, **Starlink Direct-to-Cell** and Flow-Liberty partnerships brought emergency SMS over satellite to Jamaica — and roughly **900+ donated Starlink kits** went to the Jamaican government and relief efforts. Satellite is becoming a genuine alternative for reaching users that terrestrial networks can't cover, especially in disaster response.

The lesson: access reaching the end user is not a single path. It's devices, carrier plans, MVNO resellers, and — increasingly — satellite links, all competing and cooperating to connect the same people.

## Common Misconceptions

### "MVNOs must own towers and spectrum"
No. MVNOs lease network capacity from an MNO — that's exactly what makes them "virtual."

### "Penetration above 100% means everyone has a phone"
No. It reflects multiple SIMs per person (dual-SIM), not one phone per person.

### "Postpaid is the standard Caribbean model"
No. Prepaid dominates, at 60–80% of subscribers in most Caribbean markets.

### "One company leads every region"
No. Apple and Samsung lead globally, but Android-friendly Chinese and region-specific brands dominate particular segments — and the exact global shares differ slightly by tracker.

## Summary

The end user is where the ecosystem becomes personal. Devices range from smartphones to feature phones, segments are shaped by data habits and price sensitivity, and carriers — split between prepaid and postpaid, with convergence bundles — provide the line into the network. The Caribbean is notably data-first and prepaid-heavy, with penetration above 100% driven by dual-SIM use, and it's adding MVNOs and satellite access to widen reach. Whether you're a student topping up data, a family sharing a hotspot, or a disaster-relief crew leaning on satellite SMS, your connection is the product of a device, a SIM, and a carrier business model all working together.

# Lesson 16 — Hera's Magic Spell
### Form 1 English B · Greek Myths · Unit 2 · *Echo and Narcissus* (part 1)

---

## The story so far

You have seen the gods being powerful and flawed — Zeus's revenge gift, Pluto's lonely theft,
the rule that would not bend. Unit 2's message is taking shape: **the gods are not perfect.**

Now the course leaves the Underworld and climbs Mount Olympus — and meets the one goddess bold
enough to curse a rival out of pure temper.

---

## 1. The silent woods

The goddesses of Olympus love to run in the woods — and here is the odd detail the story wants
you to notice first: the woods are **silent**.

- **Queen Hera** passes by "soundless as the sun's rays."
- **Diana** (the goddess of the hunt — the Romans' name for **Artemis**) is "quiet as
  moonlight."
- The **wood nymphs** flit about "like thistledown" — delicate, graceful, silent.

Into this perfect, silent world bursts **Echo**.

---

## 2. Echo — the nymph who must have the last word

Echo is a **wood nymph** — a spirit of nature. And she has exactly one flaw, but it is the
flaw that will wreck her life:

> **She always has to have the last word.**

Where the other goddesses move soundlessly, Echo is "always chattering, arguing or shrieking
with laughter." The deer scatter the moment she opens her mouth. Even when she is agreeing,
she cannot stop.

Watch how the story shows it:

> "What? Didn't do anything," said Echo pertly.
> …
> "Not," said Echo, who always had to have the last word.
> "Not, not, not."

She cannot let a sentence end without adding *her* words to it. It is a tiny, annoying habit —
and it is about to be punished as if it were a crime.

---

## 3. Hera's curse

Queen Hera is strolling through her silent woods, minding her own business — and Echo cannot
resist. She tags along, chirping, arguing, getting the last word in, "not, not, not."

Hera's patience snaps.

She turns on Echo, points a **magic finger** — and pronounces the curse:

> "Once and for all, be silent!"

Echo opens her mouth to argue — and no sound comes out.

Not mute exactly — worse. From that moment, Echo can **only repeat the last
words she hears**. She can never start a sentence of her own again. The chatty nymph is sealed
inside the very habit that got her cursed: whatever anyone says to her, the only words she can
ever say back are their own.

---

## 4. What this myth explains

Every myth in this unit explains something about the natural world. This one explains a sound
you have all heard — in a gym, a stairwell, a tunnel, a big empty room:

> **The echo.**

Say "Hello!" down a long corridor and the place answers your last words back to you. Stand in
a canyon and shout — the cliffs call your ending back. The ancient Greeks had to explain this
weird trick of sound, and so they told the story of a nymph condemned to repeat other people's
words forever. The voice you hear bouncing back at you? That is **Echo**, still stuck with her
curse.

---

## 5. Don't act hastily — you might regret it later

Here is the lesson the course wants you to take from this part of the story — and it sits with
**Hera**, not Echo:

> **Don't act hastily — you might regret it later.**
> *(And vanity can lead to destruction — the second half of the lesson belongs to Narcissus,
> whom you meet next lesson.)*

Hera is the goddess of marriage and family. Her curse is delivered in a single flash of temper.
She does not warn, she does not cool down — she just strikes. It feels great for about three
seconds... and then it cannot be taken back.

That is the whole of it: **hasty anger makes permanent mistakes.** Hera has just made one.
The story will not let her forget it — the goddesses who walk her woods will now listen to her
regret carried back on their own words.

---

## 6. The tail of the trap

Before this lesson ends, notice one more fold in the story's design:

- Echo is cursed by a goddess acting in a furious blink.
- But Echo's **fatal flaw** is not annoying Hera — it is the last-word habit itself.
- The curse does not remove the flaw. It *keeps* it forever: she will always be responding, but
  never speaking.

A punishment that fits the crime so neatly that it looks like the crime itself — that is what
the gods do best. And here is the seed of the tragedy you will read next lesson: the only
person whose words Echo will ever need to say again is the one person she will fall in love
with — and be unable to tell.

---

### Key terms for this lesson
- **Hera** — queen of the gods, goddess of marriage and family.
- **Echo** — the chatty wood nymph who can only repeat the last words she hears.
- **Wood nymph** — a spirit of nature (woods, rivers, trees).
- **Curse** — a magical punishment imposed by a god.
- **"To have the last word"** — to insist on the final say in an argument.

### Things to remember
- Echo's flaw: she must always have the last word.
- Hera's curse: "Once and for all, be silent!" — she can only repeat what she hears.
- The myth explains where echoes come from.
- Lesson one of two: don't act hastily — anger makes permanent mistakes.
- Lesson two arrives with Narcissus (vanity leads to destruction).

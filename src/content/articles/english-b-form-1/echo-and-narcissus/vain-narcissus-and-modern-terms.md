# Lesson 17 — Vain Narcissus & The Words We Borrow
### Form 1 English B · Greek Myths · Unit 2 · *Echo and Narcissus* (part 2)

---

## The story so far

Echo is cursed: she can speak only the last words she hears. The chatty nymph who loved the
last word is now locked inside other people's sentences. Hera has already begun to suspect she
acted too hastily.

Now a new character walks into the silent woods — and Echo's curse is about to become a
tragedy.

---

## 1. Narcissus — the boy who is too beautiful for his own good

The woods are visited by **Narcissus**, a young man so beautiful that everyone who meets him
loves him instantly. And that is exactly the problem:

> **Narcissus loves nobody back — nobody but himself.**

He is vain. He is cold. He treats the people who adore him with careless cruelty. Echo sees
him and falls hopelessly, silently in love — and her curse means she can do nothing about it.
She can only dog his footsteps in silence, hoping, watching, waiting.

---

## 2. The cruel rejection

One day Echo cannot bear it any longer. She finds a way to speak — not her words, but his.
When Narcissus calls, she gives him back his own call. When he says "stop," she says "stop."
She pours all her love into echoing him back.

Narcissus understands what is happening — and he is merciless:

> "Stupid girl. I suppose you think you love me."
> …
> "You bore me. Leave me alone!"
> …
> "Oh, do go away! I hate the sight of you. Do you really suppose I could ever care for a
> stick-insect like you? Look at yourself!"

He is not just rejecting her. He is **insulting her for being in love with him** — comparing
her to a stick-insect and telling her to look at herself.

---

## 3. Echo's vanishing — and her wicked, wordless wish

Echo's heart breaks. She grows thin and pale. She wanders into the forest to be alone.

And there she makes a **wish** — the story calls it a "wicked, wordless wish." Because she has
no words, the wish is shaped not from her mouth but from her pain:

> She wished that Narcissus should one day love as she loved him — and suffer for it as she was
> suffering.

Then Echo fades. The text is precise about it: she grows thinner and thinner, paler and paler,
until at last her **body fades away altogether. Only her voice is left**, blowing about with
the leaves.

Echo does not die like we die. She is unpicked — body gone, voice remaining. From now on, every
whisper, every call across a valley, carries her last words to somebody else.

---

## 4. Two words the myth gives to the modern world

Here is why *Echo and Narcissus* is one of the most useful stories you will meet this term.
The Greeks gave the two ruined characters' names to ordinary modern English — as words we use
almost every day:

> **Narcissist / narcissism** — excessive self-love; a person so absorbed in themselves they
> cannot see anyone else. Psychologists still use the word today, named after this very story.

> **Echo chamber** — a situation (especially online) where you only ever hear your own opinions
> repeated back to you. Scroll a social-media feed that only shows you things you already agree
> with: that is an echo chamber, named for the nymph who could only repeat other people's words.

Put them together and a strange thing appears: the two victims of the myth are walking around
in our heads every day — one as an insult to self-absorption, the other as a warning about the
spaces we build around our own opinions.

---

## 5. Why you will remember NARCISSUS — the name test

Here is the memory-hook the class uses for tests:

- **NARCISSUS** — a boy — loves his own **REFLECTION**. ("Narcissus" and "narcissism" share the
  boy's name.)
- **ECHO** — a nymph — can only **REPEAT** what she hears. ("Echo chamber" shares the nymph's
  name.)

One story, and you have two modern words for the price of one myth.

---

## 6. The trap of the story so far

Notice the shape this is taking — it is the shape the gods keep drawing in Unit 2:

- **Hera** acted hastily → this lesson, the results are already permanent.
- **Echo** faded for love → but she is not the villain; she is the victim of cruelty and a
  curse.
- **Narcissus** was cruel to someone who loved him → and the story has already planted the seed
  (his "wicked, wordless wish") of what will happen to him next.

Lesson 18 answers the obvious question: *will someone love Narcissus the way Echo loved him —
and will it destroy him?*

### Key terms for this lesson
- **Narcissus** — the vain, beautiful young man who loves only himself.
- **Vain / vanity** — proud and self-absorbed, especially about looks.
- **Unrequited love** — love that is not returned; love for someone who does not love you back.
- **Pine away** — waste away from longing or sadness.
- **Narcissist / narcissism** — excessive self-love (named after the myth).
- **Echo chamber** — hearing only your own opinions echoed back (named after the myth).

### Things to remember
- Narcissus rejects Echo cruelly — and insults her in the process.
- Echo fades away until only her voice remains.
- Her "wicked, wordless wish": that Narcissus love and suffer as she did.
- The myth's names have become modern words: narcissist and echo chamber.
- The story has set the trap for Narcissus — Lesson 18 spring it.

# Lesson 11 — The Great Zeus Debate: Just King or Cruel, Vindictive Ruler?
### Form 1 English B · Greek Myths · Unit 1

---

## Open with the motion

Today you argue, with evidence:

> **This house believes that Zeus is a cruel, vindictive ruler — not a just king.**

The two sides will be assigned: **Government** argues *for* the motion (Zeus is cruel and
vindictive); **Opposition** argues *against* it (Zeus is a just king). You may not personally
believe your side. That is normal — and it is the point. Debate is a sport of *evidence*, not
a test of sincerity.

---

## 1. The evidence you have already collected

Everything this unit taught you is now admissible as evidence. Your weapons are the quotes you
mined in Lesson 10.

**For "cruel, vindictive":**
- Zeus builds Pandora as a punishment — a "reward" that is really a trap, delivered "with a grin
  on his face."
- He chains Prometheus to a cliff forever, with eagles pecking at him — a punishment that can
  never end, because gods cannot die.
- He is jealous of creatures who merely *look like* gods.
- He releases the nine evils into the human world and shows no sign of regret.

**For "just king":**
- He gave order to creation: he sent the brothers to make living creatures, and the world was
  briefly perfect.
- He did not wipe humanity out entirely — Prometheus and Man survive.
- Hope survives the box — in the course's wording, kept inside to comfort humans and even
  Prometheus on his cliff.
- The reason the "mercy" evidence is weak is the *tension itself* — a god who is genuinely and
  simultaneously both.

**The character card (one line from the unit's own slides):** Zeus is *impulsive, jealous,
vengeful and childish — and merciful.* Note: the *same* source gives both sides. That is the
beautiful mess you are debating.

---

## 2. How the debate runs (the format)

For a class of ~19 per group:

1. **Opening statements** — Government has 1–2 minutes, Opposition has 1–2 minutes. Chosen
   speakers set out their strongest claim + evidence.
2. **Floor debate** — the argument opens up. Speakers follow the **talking-token rule**: hold
   the token to speak; safe pass if you need think-time; everyone whose hand is up gets a turn.
3. **Rebuttals** — 30–60 seconds per side: attack the *weakest* point the other side made.
   "Quote or concede" applies: if Opposition quotes a mercy scene, Government must meet it, not
   dodge it.
4. **Closing statements** — 30 seconds each side, summarising the *one* point that survived.

**Judging:** the teacher is the judge, helped by two student **evidence checkers** — their whole
job is tracking whether each claim was backed by a quote. If a speaker makes a claim without
evidence, the checker can ring them on it. (This job suits high-fliers; it is the same skill as
Lesson 10's.)

---

## 3. Winning well: what "winning" actually means

A newcomer to debate thinks winning means having the louder voice. In this class it means two
things:

1. **Quoted evidence wins over emotion.** The side that links every claim to the text wins the
   argument — even when the votes feel close.
2. **Acknowledging the other side is a weapon, not a surrender.** The best rebuttal is:
   *"Yes, Opposition — Zeus let Hope survive. But notice WHO he did not spare: Prometheus, who
   loved mankind. Mercy that only survives as a loophole is not justice."* — That sentence does
   three things: accepts the opponent's evidence, reframes it, and pushes the argument forward.

---

## 4. Preparing your speaker (a handy mini-planner)

If you are opening or closing, build your speech with the P-E-E spine:

- **Claim** (1 sentence): "Zeus is a cruel ruler, and here is the proof."
- **Evidence 1** (quote + moment): "When he hands Pandora over as 'a reward', the narrator
  notes he does it 'with a grin on his face'."
- **Explain the link**: "Grinning at a wedding you have rigged to ruin the world is not the
  behaviour of a just king — it is the pleasure of a cruel one."
- **One sentence the other side must answer**: "So tell me, Opposition — if Zeus were truly
  just, why did his wedding gift come with a lock?"

---

## 5. After the debate (what you should have won)

Win or lose, by the end of the period you will have done the unit's core work out loud:

- Made claims and defended them with quotes (**claim → quote → explain** in oral form).
- Heard the *same* evidence used for opposite purposes and seen how framing decides cases.
- Practised the respect rule: unkindness forfeits a point. Disagree with the *argument*, never
  the person.

And you will be ready for Test 1 — because every test answer you write from now on is just a
debate you have with yourself, on paper, with quotes on both sides of the page.

---

### Key terms for this lesson
- **Motion** — the proposition being debated.
- **Government / Opposition** — for / against the motion.
- **Rebuttal** — answering the opponent's point with a counter-point or stronger evidence.
- **Evidence checker** — the student role that enforces "quote or concede."

### Things to remember
- You argue the side assigned — that is the discipline.
- Every claim needs a quote; every quote needs an explanation.
- The best rebuttals accept the opponent's evidence and re-frame it.
- Disagree with arguments, never with people.

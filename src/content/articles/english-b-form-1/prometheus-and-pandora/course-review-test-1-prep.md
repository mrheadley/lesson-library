# Lesson 12 — Course Review: Everything Unit 1 Needs You to Own
### Form 1 English B · Greek Myths · Unit 1

---

## Why this lesson exists

Tomorrow you sit **Test 1** — on the Nature of Myths, Greek vs. Biblical Creation, and Pandora's
Box. This lesson is the lap around the track before the race: same ground, faster. Do not just
read it — *use* it. If you can answer the questions in each section out loud, from memory, you
are ready.

---

## 1. The definition of a myth (say it from memory)

> **A myth is a traditional story that expresses a culture's beliefs and values, and explains
> the things it cannot yet explain by science — human origins, good and evil, life and death,
> the afterlife, and the gods.**

Also keep the three jobs: a myth **explains**, a myth **teaches**, a myth **preserves** a
culture's values.

**Revision question:** What is a myth *not*? (Answer: a myth is not a "lie" — it is an
explanation and a lesson.)

---

## 2. The Greek god family (the lineage)

Say the chain out loud:

**Mother Earth (Gaia) → Father Heaven (Ouranos) → The Titans (Cronus) → The Olympian gods
(Zeus, Hades, Poseidon, Hera, Demeter, Hestia)**

Now the three realms of the sons:
- **Hades** → the **Underworld**
- **Poseidon** → the **Sea**
- **Zeus** → the **Heaven** (and kingship over the gods)

**Revision questions:** Who was the first being? What did Father Heaven do to his children? What
did Cronus do to his father? What *happened to Cronus* when he gained power? (The cycle of power:
each ruler repeats the cruelty he overthrew.)

---

## 3. The Pandora arc (the narrative spine)

The story moves in five beats. Re-sequence these from memory:

1. **Creation & gifts** — Epimetheus makes the animals; Prometheus moulds First Man from mud so
   he looks up at the stars; Prometheus steals a sliver of fire from the sun's chariot.
2. **The punishment planned** — Zeus chains Prometheus (eagles, forever) and secretly builds
   Pandora with Venus, Mercury, and Apollo's gifts.
3. **The forbidden chest** — Pandora is given to Epimetheus as a "reward"; the wedding present
   is an iron-bound chest they must not open.
4. **The opening** — curiosity wins; the nine evils are released (Disease, Cruelty, Pain, Old
   Age, Disappointment, Hate, Jealousy, War, Death).
5. **Hope survives** — one white, butterfly-like flicker remains (outline wording) / is
   released (core-text wording), to keep humans — and even Prometheus — going.

**Revision question:** Which stage of the arc does the quote "what harm can that do?" belong to,
and what is dangerous about it? (Stage 4 — the moment curiosity is talked into opening the chest.)

## 4. The two creation stories, compared (the A1 homework you already wrote)

| | Greek account | Biblical account (Genesis 1–3) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Who creates?** | A family of flawed gods | One good God |
| **How are humans made?** | Moulded from mud by Prometheus | Formed from dust by God, given life |
| **How does evil come in?** | Pandora opens a forbidden box | Human disobedience in the garden |
| **After the fall?** | Hope survives to comfort | Judgement, promise, redemption ahead |

**Revision question:** Name one similarity and one difference — then turn them into one
"comparison sentence" (similarity + difference + insight).

---

## 5. The evidence habit (claim → quote → explain)

For any character question tomorrow, run the three moves:

1. **Claim** — one arguable sentence.
2. **Quote** — the shortest phrase that proves it.
3. **Explain** — why the quote proves the claim.

Practise on: *"Zeus is a cruel ruler."* and *"Zeus shows mercy."* You should be able to supply
quotes for **both** — that reflex is worth marks.

---

## 6. The character cards to know by name

- **Zeus** — king of the gods; impulsive, jealous, vengeful, and merciful; created Pandora as a
  punishment.
- **Prometheus** — "forethought"; Titan; moulded First Man; stole fire; chained to a cliff.
- **Epimetheus** — "afterthought"; made the animals; accepted Pandora; never suspects tricks.
- **Pandora** — first woman; assembled by the gods; opened the forbidden chest.
- **Venus / Aphrodite** — goddess of love and beauty; gave Pandora beauty.
- **Mercury / Hermes** — clever messenger god; gave Pandora her clever tongue.
- **Apollo** — god of music and light; taught Pandora sweet music.

## 7. The morals that glue it together

Never lose hope, even when everything seems negative. Obey those in authority. Be careful of
gifts from enemies. Curiosity can have dangerous consequences. Actions have consequences.

Ask any character or event from Unit 1 "what does this story teach?" and the answer connects to
these four lines.

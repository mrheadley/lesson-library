# Summative-03 Review — Test 3: Perseus, Heracles, Theseus, and The Wooden Horse
### Form 1 English B · Greek Myths · Unit 3 · Self-study review (read before the test)

---

## What Test 3 covers

Test 3 (Week 12, Lesson 12.3 — slot 36) covers the hero-era of Unit 3:

1. **Perseus** — the prophecy, the chest, Medusa's head, Andromeda, the statues.
2. **The Twelve Labours of Heracles** — the broken promise, the penance, the clever labours.
3. **Theseus and the Minotaur** — the thread, the dark fight, the black sail.
4. **The Wooden Horse of Troy** — the theft of Helen, the ten-year siege, the gift-trap.

The test wants what Tests 1 and 2 wanted: **the facts cold**, **the evidence habit** (every
claim backed by a quote), and the new thread of this unit articulated: **Greek heroes win by
cleverness, not just strength — and even then, their flaws and carelessness cost them.**

---

## 1. Know the four stories in the big beats

Run these as flashcards until they snap:

- **Perseus:** prophecy to Acrisius → chest on the sea → Dictys saves → Polydectes's dare
  (Medusa's head) → divine gifts (Hermes' sandals, Athene's mirror-shield, Pluto's helmet +
  bag) → Zeus "watch, but not help" → Grey Sisters' eye → backward flight + reflection →
  beheading asleep Medusa → the bagged head → Andromeda + sea serpent → wedding = 50 statues →
  Polydectes + 500 statues → the mis-thrown discus kills Acrisius → **"Nobody can cheat
  Fate."**
- **Heracles:** *Hercules* is the Roman name → snakes in the crib → the schoolmaster's warning
  → one glass of wine → family dead → 7 years' service to Eurystheus → Lion (worn as a skin) →
  Hydra (cut + singe = no regrow) → stag, boar (name them) → Augean stables (dam the river) →
  Atlas and the apples (the pad-for-the-shoulders trick) → free, but never free of the sorrow →
  set among the stars. **Idiom: "a Herculean task."**
- **Theseus:** Athens's tribute of 14 to feed the Minotaur → Theseus volunteers → white sail /
  black sail promise → Ariadne's thread → the fight in utter darkness → wounded, string
  knocked away → kills the beast, finds the thread → abandons Ariadne on the island →
  forgets the sail → Aegeus throws himself into the sea → the **Aegean Sea**. **Question:
  "Hero or not?"**
- **The Wooden Horse:** Helen "hatched from an egg" → Paris steals her → fifty thousand + a
  thousand ships → ten years deciding nothing (Achilles, Ajax, Paris dead) → Odysseus's plan →
  the hollow horse, a **dozen** soldiers → "A tribute to Troy! A tribute to the City of
  Horses!" → the old man's spear + warning → towed in through the gates → Helen suspects,
  stays silent → midnight door → gates opened → the fleet returns → Troy burns. **Idioms:
  Trojan Horse + "Beware of Greeks bearing gifts."**

## 2. The three reasons you will lose marks on Test 3

- **Variants.** The unit again kept deliberate variant details. Know both sides — the rubric
  rewards naming the version, not choosing a "winner":
  - **Heracles vs. Hercules** — Greek vs. Roman name; the course uses Heracles.
  - **Ted: the wedding / the palace counts** — 50 spearmen frozen at the wedding vs. *five
    hundred* soldiers at Polydectes's court. Keep the numbers separate.
  - **Helen** — the course text says Paris "stole Helen's love" (theft); an older legend has
    her going willingly. Course version = theft.
- **Facts, not fluency.** "Perseus used a shield" is thin; the chain is: flew *backwards*,
   holding Athene's shield up as a mirror, so he could *see her reflected without meeting her
  gaze*, then struck while she slept. Retell in order.
- **Opinion without a quote.** "Theseus is careless" is not an answer; "Theseus forgot the
  faith of the black sail — *'he quite forgot to change the black sail for a white one'* — and
  his father died for it, so yes, careless" is an answer.

## 3. The "unit theme" question (high-value, plan for it)

A question like *"How do the heroes of this unit win — and what do they lose?"* is asking for
the thread that runs through all four stories:

1. **Cleverness beats strength.** Perseus wins with a mirror and a bagged head; Heracles
   singes the Hydra, dams the stables, tricks Atlas; Theseus survives by a ball of string;
   Troy falls to a hollow gift. Quote any one.
2. **The gods help — but they test.** Zeus watches rather than saves Perseus; the labours are
   the price of a broken promise; the gods finish Heracles's story among the stars.
3. **Flaws cost.** Theseus's carelessness kills his father; Heracles's pride (one glass)
   destroys his family; Troy's arrogance ignores its own warning. Every win in the unit has a
   loss tucked beside it.

Each of those three moves can be supplied with a **Claim → Quote → Explain** from any story.

## 4. The modern words and idioms (a slam-dunk section)

Slam-dunk, but keep the *exact* meanings:

- **A Herculean task** — an enormously difficult job (from Heracles's labours).
- **Trojan Horse** — a deceptive trap that looks like a gift (also the computer malware).
- **"Beware of Greeks bearing gifts"** — don't trust an enemy's kindness.
- **(Bonus, carry from the term) the Midas touch, narcissist, echo chamber, arachnid, opening
  Pandora's box, Achilles' heel.**

## 5. The one-question self-test (do this tonight)

Answer in three sentences, using the **Claim → Quote → Explain** spine:

**"Heracles is the strongest man in the world. Why does 'the strongest' need cunning?"**

A strong answer runs: *Heracles's victories come from changing the rules, not lifting harder —
Claim — because on the Hydra the text shows him "sing[ing] the ragged end with his red-hot
club" so the head "did not regrow" — Quote. Even the mightiest hero must think: strength alone
feeds the Hydra; fire beats it — Explain.*

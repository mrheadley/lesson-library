# Lesson 39 — The Whole Term, One Game
### Form 1 English B · Greek Myths · Unit 3 · *Interactive Term Review*

---

## The story so far

Your final exam sits two lessons away. You have read eleven myths, seven journal reflections,
and one genuine arc from creation to unchaining. This lesson turns all of it into a game —
your last rehearsal before the real paper.

Here is everything the review needs you to carry: the eleven stories, the character shelf, the
idioms with modern footprints — and the exact shape of the exam you're walking into.

---

## 1. The eleven stories, in order (the skeleton of the term)

Memorise this sequence. Almost every exam question will ask you to *place* a story, a hero, or
a moral anywhere along it:

1. **What is a Myth? / In the Beginning & Pandora's Box** (Weeks 1–3) — mud-men, the stolen
   fire, the nine evils, the butterfly **Hope**; the chaining of Prometheus.
2. **Persephone and the Pomegranate Seeds** (Week 5) — the six seeds, the six-month pact, the
   seasons explained.
3. **Echo and Narcissus** (Week 6) — the cursed nymph; the vain boy; "echo chamber,"
   "narcissist."
4. **Daedalus and Icarus** (Week 7) — the labyrinth, the wax-and-wool wings, **hubris** and the
   fall.
5. **Arachne the Spinner** (Week 8) — the weaving contest, the spider curse, "arachnid."
6. **King Midas** (Week 9) — donkey ears, the golden touch, the river cure, "the Midas touch."
7. **Perseus** (Week 10) — the chest, Medusa's head, the sea serpent, the discus, "nobody can
   cheat Fate."
8. **The Twelve Labours of Heracles** (Week 11) — the drink promise, the lion, the Hydra, the
   river-stables, tricking Atlas, "a Herculean task."
9. **Theseus and the Minotaur** (Week 12) — the thread, the dark fight, the black sail, the
   Aegean Sea.
10. **The Wooden Horse** (Week 12) — ten years, a dozen soldiers, "Beware of Greeks bearing
    gifts," "Trojan Horse."
11. **Freedom for Prometheus** (Week 13) — the older, wiser Zeus; pliers of lightning; the
    cycle broken.

**Review tip:** quiz yourself by picking *any one* of the eleven and giving, out loud, its hero,
its plot in three sentences, and its theme in one sentence. That's a rehearsal of exactly what
Paper 1 will ask.

## 2. Idioms with modern footprints (Paper 2 gold)

The exam's second paper loves these — the Greek phrases that walked straight into English:

| Idiom | Meaning | Born from |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Midas touch** | a golden knack for profit — with a hidden cost | King Midas |
| **Herculean task** | an enormous, almost impossible job | Heracles's labours |
| **Trojan Horse** | a deceptive trap that looks like a gift | the wooden horse of Troy |
| **Beware of Greeks bearing gifts** | don't trust an enemy's kindness | the old man's warning at Troy |
| **Echo chamber** | a space where you only hear your own words back | poor Echo |
| **Narcissist** | someone in love with their own reflection | Narcissus |
| **Arachnid** | the spider family science calls it | Arachne's curse |
| **Opening Pandora's box** | unleashing a flood of problems | the box of the nine evils |
| **Achilles' heel** | a single fatal weakness | the invincible Greek's one flaw |

Keep your wording tight: a "Herculean task" is *a huge hard job*, not "a famous thing"; a
"Trojan Horse" is *a deceptive trap*, not just "a big statue." The exam will test edge-slippage
like that.

## 3. The character shelf (who belongs to which story)

- **Gods:** Zeus, Hera, Pluto/Hades, Athene, Hermes/Mercury, Apollo, Demeter, Aphrodite,
  Poseidon (Troy's patron — the "City of Horses" link).
- **Titans:** Prometheus, Epimetheus (afterthought), Atlas, Gaia.
- **Heroes:** Perseus, Heracles/Hercules, Theseus, Odysseus, Achilles (the heel), Ajax.
- **The supporting cast who matter:** Danaë, Polydectes, Dictys, Acrisius, Andromeda
  (Perseus); Eurystheus, Augeas, Atlas (Heracles); Ariadne, Minos, Aegeus, the Minotaur
  (Theseus); Menelaus, Paris, Agamemnon, Priam, Helen (Troy).

**And spell them right** — the outline's spelling list is a Paper 2 target. Slow words first:
**Ariadne, Acrisius, Eurystheus, Augean, Persephone, Andromeda, Minotaur.**

## 4. "Myths change in the telling" — the variants to settle

The course has shown you that no myth has one fixed text. Before the exam, know which version
*your* course text uses:

- **Heracles / Hercules** — Greek name vs. Roman name; the course uses Heracles.
- **Pluto / Hades**, **Hermes / Mercury** — same gods, Greek vs. Roman names.
- **Helen** — stolen (course text) or willing (old legend); the class text says Paris "stole
  Helen's love."
- **The satyr / centaur** — the battle of donkey-ears vs. the half-man half-horse; the course's
  Midas tells you which.
- **The freeing of Prometheus** — Heracles' arrow in the standard myth; **the older, wiser Zeus
  with pliers of lightning in this course's text.** The course version wins on the paper.

The exam may *ask* you to name a variant — being able to say "in the other telling, Heracles
frees Prometheus; in our text, Zeus does" is a guaranteed-marks answer.

## 5. The shape of the final exam (two papers)

The outline's Week 14 structure:

- **Paper 1 — writing:** character, plot, and theme. Rehearse the habit: "hero in a sentence,
  plot in order, theme in one sentence" for all eleven.
- **Paper 2 — closed questions:** multiple choice, vocabulary, idioms, spelling. The Kahoot
  format of this lesson is its mirror image — the drill *is* the rehearsal.

No item counts are published; prepare for the *shape*, not a fixed number. Anything you can
do in this review game, you can do on the paper.

---

## 6. The image to keep in your pocket

**One timeline with a chained Titan at one end, a free man at the other, and eleven little
stories slotted along the curve between them.**

Everything on the exam lives on that line. Place the story → get the hero → get the idiom →
get the moral. Master the timeline, and Paper 1 and Paper 2 nearly take care of themselves.

### Key terms for this lesson (the review bank)
- **The eleven stories in term order** — the skeleton of every exam answer (list above).
- **Nine modern-footprint idioms** — table above.
- **Variant** — another version of a myth; know the course's choice in each clash.
- **Paper 1 / Paper 2** — the exam's two shapes: analysis writing vs. closed questions.
- **Kahoot!** — the review game; speed-round, low-stakes, nobody watches anyone's score.

### Things to remember
- Eleven stories, in order, with hero/plot/theme ready for any one chosen at random.
- Idioms must keep their *exact* meanings — no "famous things" slippage.
- Spelling list: Ariadne, Acrisius, Eurystheus, Augean, Persephone, Andromeda, Minotaur.
- Settle the variants with the course text: Heracles-not-Hercules, Zeus-frees-Prometheus,
  Helen-stolen.
- Exam = Paper 1 (analysis writing) + Paper 2 (MC / vocabulary / idioms / spelling).

# Summative-04 Review — End-of-Term Exam: The Whole Syllabus
### Form 1 English B · Greek Myths · Unit 1–3 · Self-study review (read before the exam)

---

## What the exam covers

The end-of-term exam (Week 14, Lessons 14.1–14.2 — slots 40–41) is **comprehensive**: every
story from Week 1 to Week 13, in two papers:

- **Paper 1 — writing:** character, plot, and theme; essays and analysis across all eleven
  studied myths.
- **Paper 2 — closed questions:** multiple choice, vocabulary, idioms, and spelling.

Everything below is the whole term on two pages. Use your lesson articles and journal
reflections alongside it.

---

## 1. The eleven stories — the full map, in order

1. **What is a Myth? / In the Beginning & Pandora's Box** — what myths do (explain + teach);
   mud-men; the stolen fire; the nine evils; the butterfly **Hope**; Prometheus chained;
   Greek vs. Genesis creation; Zeus's early character (impulsive, jealous, vengeful, yet
   merciful).
2. **Persephone and the Pomegranate Seeds** — the abduction; the six seeds; the six-month
   pact; seasons explained; Pluto's loneliness; Demeter's grief.
3. **Echo and Narcissus** — the curse (repeat only); the vain boy; the pool; **narcissist** +
   **echo chamber**.
4. **Daedalus and Icarus** — the labyrinth; the flock of feathers; the warning; hubris; the
   fall; obedience.
5. **Arachne the Spinner** — the boast; the contest; the satire; the spider curse;
   **arachnid**.
6. **King Midas** — the biased verdict; donkey ears; the barber's hole; the golden touch; the
   river cure; **the Midas touch**.
7. **Perseus** — the prophecy; the chest; the dare; the gifts; the Grey Sisters' eye; Medusa;
   Andromeda; 50 + 500 statues; the discus; **"Nobody can cheat Fate."**
8. **The Twelve Labours of Heracles** — the promise; the family; 7 years of penance;
   Eurystheus; lion, Hydra, stables, Atlas; the stars; **a Herculean task**.
9. **Theseus and the Minotaur** — the tribute; the thread; the darkness; Ariadne; the black
   sail; the **Aegean Sea**; "Hero or not?"
10. **The Wooden Horse of Troy** — Helen; the ten-year siege; the hollow horse; a dozen
    soldiers; "a tribute to the City of Horses"; **Trojan Horse** + "Beware of Greeks bearing
    gifts."
11. **Freedom for Prometheus** — "Even the gods grow wiser as they grow older"; the pliers of
    lightning; the eagles spun away; free "to champion the little people"; the cycle broken.

## 2. The four term themes (Paper 1's essay bank)

Every Paper 1 question that works is a variation of these four, and each can be answered with
**Claim → Quote → Explain** using any story:

1. **Myths explain the world and teach a lesson.** The seasons (Persephone), spiders
   (Arachne), the narcissus flower, the Aegean's name, the echo — and the moral of every
   story: respect the gods, actions have consequences, hope should never be lost.
2. **The Greek gods are powerful but flawed.** Impulsive, jealous, vengeful, childish — yet
   merciful (Hope remains; Hera regrets; Zeus grows). Compare against Unit 1's "fundamentally
   good" God (Genesis, **Jeremiah 29:11**).
3. **Heroes win by cleverness, and pay for their flaws.** Perseus's mirror, Heracles's fire
   and river, Theseus's thread, Odysseus's horse — all brains over brawn — and all four cost
   something: Fate's discus, a lifelong sorrow, a father's suicide, a burned city.
4. **The cycle of justice breaks when the punisher grows.** Every punishment in the term bred
   more punishment — until the older, wiser Zeus clipped the chains. The whole syllabus is one
   arc: creation → fire → chaining → Pandora → Hope → hero-era → unchaining.

## 3. The idioms and name-loans (Paper 2's engine room)

- **Opening Pandora's box** — unleashing a flood of trouble.
- **Narcissist / narcissism** — excessive self-love (from Narcissus).
- **Echo chamber** — hearing only your own opinions back (from Echo).
- **The Midas touch** — everything you touch succeeds financially.
- **A Herculean task** — a gigantic, near-impossible job.
- **Achilles' heel** — a single fatal weakness.
- **Trojan Horse** — a deceptive trap disguised as a gift (and the computer malware).
- **"Beware of Greeks bearing gifts"** — don't trust an enemy's kindness.
- *(Extras worth a word: arachnid; "heroes and heroines among those men and women.")*

## 4. The variants the paper may test (know the course's version)

- **Heracles / Hercules** — Greek vs. Roman name. Course: Heracles.
- **Pluto / Hades**, **Hermes / Mercury** — same gods, two names.
- **Helen** — stolen (course text) vs. willing (older legend). Course: theft.
- **Midas's ears and wish** — satyr/centaur variant; the course's Midas is the ass-eared,
  golden-touched one.
- **Who frees Prometheus** — Heracles' arrow (standard myth) vs. **the older, wiser Zeus**
  with pliers of lightning (course text). Course: Zeus.
- **Colouring of Zeus's character** — PPT named him impulsive/jealous/vengeful *and* merciful;
  the closing story is where the "merciful" wins.

## 5. The Paper-1 formula that never fails

For any story, any question:

1. **Hero** in one sentence — "Perseus, son of Zeus and Danaë, the hero who…"
2. **Plot in order** — the big beats only, no decoration.
3. **Theme in one sentence** — "…shows that nobody can cheat Fate."
4. **One quote** that *proves* the theme — and one sentence explaining why the quote proves it.

That is the claim–quote–explain spine the whole term drilled. On the paper, it is the whole
game.

## 6. The one-question self-test (do this the night before)

Answer in three sentences, using the **Claim → Quote → Explain** spine:

**"If you had to explain the Greeks with ONE story, which would you choose — and what would it
teach?"**

A strong answer runs: *I'd choose Freedom for Prometheus, because it holds the whole term's
arc in one frame — Claim — the story's key line is "even the gods grow wiser as they grow
older," and its hero pinions are the pliers of lightning that clip the chains — Quote. It
teaches that power, flawed at first, can become merciful — and that love for the little people
outlasts punishment — Explain.*
